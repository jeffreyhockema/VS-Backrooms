# Changelog

## 0.514.0 — the graphics pass

Every texture and model the mod adds was reviewed in the game against the vanilla blocks beside it, level by level, and rebuilt where it fell short. Nothing vanilla changed, no block or item codes changed, and existing worlds keep working: new looks apply to every block already placed, and the few props with new shapes (Level 9's roofs and gables, Level 94's water towers) appear in chunks generated from now on.

In numbers: 1,219 block textures (611 of them new versions, so a surface no longer repeats block by block), 59 creature and character textures, 62 item icons and 608 block models were redrawn or rebuilt across all 22 levels. At the start 180 asset families were graded against vanilla and none matched it; at the end 196 families were compared side by side, before and after, and all but one were kept (13 after a tweak), the exception being Level 2's concrete tunnel walls, which went back to their old look.

- Surfaces: wallpapers, carpets, concrete, brick, tile, plaster, asphalt, turf and marble are built from a shared material kit with real structure and shading, tile without visible block edges, and no longer repeat the same mark on every block.
- Hardware is geometry, not paint: gauges, dials, grilles, door handles, kick plates, hinges, breaker panels, cable trays, lamps, chandeliers, sconces, elevator doors and call buttons.
- Furniture is modelled: desks, office chairs, filing cabinets, diner booths, store shelves stocked with packs, food-court chairs and tables, arcade cabinets, vending machines, the hospital bed, the Fort Surrender furniture.
- Level 1: poured concrete floors and formed walls, a new camp kit (tent, sleeping bag, burn barrel), framed crates and a looted crate.
- Level 2: machinery cabinets with real dials, the dead tram with recessed windows and a grille, modelled steel doors.
- Level 3: breaker panels, fans, cable trays, risers, a riveted boiler, switchgear and transformer, enamel plaques, the Sanctum's ashlar and statue.
- Level 5: panelled hotel doors with brass plates, an eight-arm chandelier, the front hall desk, key rack and stairs, the cage lift.
- Level 9: gables carry the house's own siding to the roof; pitched shingle roofs with eaves, gutters and ridge caps; porch posts, front doors, mailboxes; fine-grained asphalt.
- Level 11: façade windows, string courses and cornices as geometry; subway tracks, tiles and signs; billboards and advertising pillars with crisp posters.
- Levels 37 and 94: shaded mosaic with recessed grout and a modelled POOL door; clay turf without the repeating swirl, the castle, water towers, fountains and milk vans.
- Level 94's water towers are rebuilt as the white municipal tower in the pictures: four braced legs on footings, a riser over the Almond Water tap, a round tank with a conical roof, a catwalk with a railing, ladders, and rust runs on the white paint. They appear in chunks generated from now on.
- Level 94, in worlds made from now on (layout version 8): every house is cream-yellow, as in the pictures (some were white), and the towns' siren poles stand at the corners across the road from the plaza (the house lots used to cover them, so the tune played from nowhere). Existing worlds keep the layout they were made with.
- Levels no longer bleed into each other at their edges, in worlds made from now on (layout version 9): each level looks like itself right up to the wall, and the wall shows each side its own face (Level 0's wallpaper on the Lobby side, Level 1's concrete on the car park's), so Level 0 no longer wears to concrete and loses ceiling tiles near Level 1, Level 1 grows no pipes near Level 2, Level 3's cables stay cables up to Level 2's wall, Level 4 no longer turns hotel red near Level 5, and the Snackrooms' mall wall no longer faces Level 6's maze. The only mixing left is in the way through a gate: Level 0's carpet is worn to the concrete in the doorway to Level 1, and Level 4's is the hotel's red in the doorway to Level 5. Existing worlds keep the layout they were made with.
- Level 1, in worlds made from now on (layout version 9): stairs are two blocks wide, as in a real car park. The stair cores' flights and landings are two wide (the cores are a row deeper, backing onto the fire wall or reaching back behind the stalls), and so are the maintenance halls' stairwells. Existing worlds keep the layout they were made with.
- Other public stairs are two wide too in new worlds (layout version 9): Level 4's office stairwells, Level 5's staff stairs and Level 11's lobby stairs. Stairs in houses and hidden stairs stay one wide.
- Level 1's car park, in new worlds (layout version 9): signs no longer stand in the driving aisles. Wet floor signs and cones stand beside the puddles, off the road; an EXIT sign by each stair core points at its doors; STOP signs stand on the corners where an aisle meets a cross aisle; the narrow aisle along each district's south wall is one way, with ONE WAY and NO ENTRY signs; and every column along a row of stalls carries the same bay letter.
- Level 1's car park, in new worlds (layout version 9): where a wall cuts a parking bay short (one block wide, or too shallow for a car), the bay is left as plain floor, with no lines, wheel stops, cars or crates.
- The boiler in Level 3's boiler rooms and Level 5's boiler bays is riveted dark iron plate with strap hoops and rust running from its seams, under a round flue; the bays' pipes up the walls are round.
- Walls with several looks (wallpaper, concrete, brick and tile, drywall, the hotel's damask, siding, barn boards, the city's façades and subway tiles, Level 7's rock) now vary block by block up a wall too, not only along it, so a stain or crack no longer stacks into a stripe.
- Level 0's ceiling lights show their lens only from below; where a light's side shows, it is the plain edge of its housing, not a second lens. Level 7's rock reads as broken facets. Kitty's House's beds, dressers, desks, chairs, table and banquettes have bevelled edges and panelled drawers; Level 94's siren poles carry horn speakers on cross arms and a junction box; Level 11's traffic lights have domed lenses and deeper visors, on both faces.
- The far places: Level !'s crash rail, doors, notices and set pieces; Level Fun's lobbies, elevator doors and signs; The End's shelving and EXIT doors; the Hub's lamps, sigils and tower; 974's rooms; 3999's marble, food court and arcade; 188's pavers, blinds and bollards.
- Going through a threshold (a door, stairs, a tripwire, a pit, a manhole, an elevator, a vending machine, a gate) now fades the screen to black the moment it fires and back in once you are put down and the place has reached you, instead of nothing happening for a few seconds while the destination is generated. Falls swoosh, stairs and tripwires have footsteps and an elevator chimes on arrival; it is a smooth fade, never a flash, and shows only for the one travelling. For admins: `/backrooms transitiontest [door|walk|fall|elevator|plain] [seconds]` plays it without travelling, and `/backrooms transitionbench x z` times how long the server takes to load a chunk column.
- Almond Water stands the right way up in the inventory (it was drawn upside down, cap at the bottom), and using it is now a drink: the bottle is raised to the mouth with the drinking sound, and the hold-to-use hint says Drink. It restores the same satiety and counts as food as before. Canned beans, crackers and The End's memory juice had the same fault and stand the right way up too.
- Level 2's plain tunnel wall keeps its dark grey tone but loses the shaded circle that sat in the middle of every block (and the tie holes and streaks that repeated with it): three versions share one soft ground, are turned and picked at random by height too, so no block edge or repeated mark shows, and one block in eight carries a faint damp run. The brick, warning and machinery walls are as before.
- Level 2's flatbed trolley is rebuilt as a model: steel channel chassis with cross members and axles, five weathered deck boards (one split and sagging), stake pockets with a bent stake still in one, a tow ring, rubber tyres on hubs, and a push handle with a grip, all rusted and grimy.
- Where a wall pipe in Levels 2 and 3 ran out at a door or a bare wall it ended in a capped flange; now it bends 90 degrees into the wall behind a union ring and a bolted wall plate, for both pipe sizes.
- Level 2's machine cabinets no longer have an open pipe stub on top: the pipe rises out of a gland and turns back into the wall above the cabinet. This shows on every cabinet already placed, in Level 3 too.
- Level 2, in worlds made from now on (layout version 9): the copper and steel conduits out of the top of the wall cabinets' gauge panels run on up the wall into the ceiling, held by clips and ending in ceiling plates. The pump galleries' machine cabinets feed the overhead pipes through a pipe up the wall behind them, and no wall cabinet hides behind a free-standing one any more.
- Level 2, in worlds made from now on: the red valves are where valves belong. A valve sits in the wall pipes as a gate valve with its hand wheel at head height (never on the bare wall below them, and never two side by side), and about one riser in three has a valve at its foot. Existing worlds keep the layout they were made with.
- Level 3, in worlds made from now on (layout version 9): its pipes, cables and gauges make sense. The wall pipes run whole round a block of wall and the room in it, inside and out, turning the corners and bending into the wall where they end, instead of coming in short pieces that stopped in mid-air (a small room had four or so). A switchgear cabinet never has a pipe coming out of its front: the pipes along the wall behind it go into its sides, and the walls beside it are left bare. The pressure gauges sit in the wall pipes, one standing on the lagged pipe and one hanging from the steel pipe, instead of on a length of pipe of their own that lined up with nothing. A cable run along a wall goes the whole length of the wall, from a panel or a gland in the wall to a gland at the other end. An electrical panel's conduit runs up out of its top into a junction box, which takes the cables of a run beside it. The cables on the floor no longer lie a block at a time: they cross a corridor from a panel or junction box, down the wall and over the floor, to a cable box or a fitting on the wall opposite. Existing worlds keep the layout they were made with.

## 0.513.3 — fixes from the first week

Multiplayer fixes, kept notes, a Spanish translation and a few repairs. Existing worlds keep working.

- Spanish translation (es-419 and es-es), by NeoB. The Spanish translation is complete and reviewed: the handbook pages and other text that changed in 0.513 are translated too.
- Fixed: breaking a torch (or anything else) you had put down yourself in the Hub's Tower counted as vandalism, and the Keymaster threw you out with nothing and closed the Tower to you for good. Only the Tower's own blocks count now; what you place there is yours to take up again.
- For admins: `/backrooms hub reset [player]` lets a player into the Tower again as if they had never been (the Keymaster offers them a key once more). In singleplayer, type it yourself. For anyone thrown out by the bug above.
- Wanderers' notes are no longer used up when read: the note stays in your pockets and remembers who has read it, so on a server it can be passed on and every player gets the next page in their own sequence from the same paper (one read per player per note; reading one again only says it is in your journal). A read note is named "(read)" to whoever has read it and stacks apart from fresh ones, which stack as before. The pocket note and notes lying loose follow the same rule. Existing notes keep working.
- Fixed: the game's own low-stability look (dark brown fog and a darkened world, which comes on whenever your Reality Stability is low, as after every Reality Shift) was laid over each level's own air and followed you through every elevator, door and stairway, so Level 4's gloom seemed to stay on in Level 5 and fog seemed to come through a door with you. The level's air now always shows; the distortion, whispers and rust still tell you your stability is low. The Mangled's fog lifts as soon as you leave the Suburbs, however you leave.
- Low Reality Stability shows in the air again, now each level's own: below 0.3 the fog of whatever level you are in thickens and turns a dull, dark brown-grey of its own colour, and the world dims by nearly a third, more the lower your stability falls. It fades in and out over a second or so, clears as your stability comes back, and lamps still light the room.
- Fixed: two players together were hunted by twice as much, and more. The director counted what was about each player only among the entities put down for them, so a pair at the edge of a level could have twelve menacing things about them where one player has six; and an entity struck by the other player became theirs, which took it off the first player's count and let the director put down more. The count is now of everything menacing about the place, whoever it was put down for. Playing alone, nothing changes.
- Fixed: a Hound stared at by the other player, a Smiler held in their gaze or a glimpse they watched took no notice: only a stare from the one it was put down for counted. Anyone's does now.
- Fixed: a hunter put down 14 blocks from one player could land right beside the other. Nothing is put down within 12 blocks of anyone now.
- Dev: `/backrooms dummies` has the director direct the straw dummies that `/backrooms encounter` puts down from the console, so what gathers round one or two players can be watched on a headless server.

## 0.513.2 — fixes

- Fixed: looking at a Level Fun party-supply shelf, a Level 3999 neon lamp or a plush crashed the game: the game's own container and orientation code rebuilt their block codes wrongly and asked for a block that does not exist. The shelves also showed missing textures, and the lamps and plushes could not be placed. A dev command, `/backrooms blocksweep`, now asks every block what the block-info HUD asks, so this cannot hide again.
- Fixed: The End's computers never went dead after a party was carried off, and Level 3999's generator never switched on: both looked for their next block by the same wrong name. Existing worlds keep working.

## 0.513.1 — fixes

Two fixes from the first reports on 0.513.0. Existing worlds keep working.

- Fixed: harvesting a Death Rat, Hound or Skin-stealer with a knife gave nothing (the body opened empty). The knife stopped after the client's 2 seconds while the server's copy of the body wanted 5, so the drops were never generated; both sides now take 2 seconds. Bodies that never had anything to give (Facelings, Smilers and the rest) no longer offer to be harvested.
- Fixed: a crash every tick (and a save that would no longer load) while a player stood in Level Fun whose player object was not attached yet, as when joining; the Partygoers' disguise check and the light and moth senses now treat such a player as empty-handed.

## 0.513.0 — first update

Fixes and balance from the first players' feedback, a mapless mode and a harsher scarcity setting. Existing worlds keep working, with the layout they were made with.

- Fixed: scrap wire, circuit boards and coin mechanisms can go in a crucible, as the handbook says.
- Fixed: the ladder down a Level 3 manhole can be climbed, all the way up through the manhole to the street.
- Every item of the mod can be set down on the ground (shift + right-click; ctrl + shift for tools, chalk and plush toys).
- For admins: the cheat menu (F7) is reorganised, with a status line, a Back button, the world settings and a Dev tab of tests.
- *Backrooms scarcity* now reaches all food: a new Scarce setting (about a third of the food); every food and drink in boxes, crates and fridges counts, not just a list; vending machines, water coolers, fountains and Kitty's fridge hold less and restock more slowly on Sparse and Scarce; Level 10 has less livestock; and new worlds on Sparse or Scarce have thinner gardens, fields and berry bushes in Levels 9 and 10 (fixed at world creation). Worlds are now created at layout version 7.
- Flint is a Level 1 find: a sealed supply box holds one about one time in twenty (was two in five), a torn one one in fifty. The glass shiv and the improvised scraper carry you through the Lobby.
- The shiv and the scraper cut wallpaper and carpet cleanly: two strips or fibres a cut, where any other knife, a flint one too, gives one.
- Metal brackets are about half as common from supply boxes, Level 1's crates, ceiling tiles, light fixtures and broken metal and wiring.
- The First Steps tutorial no longer asks for flint in the Lobby: steps 11 to 13 are now the scraper, tinder and a torch, and the last step points to Level 1 for flint. Progress is kept, and anyone who had made the flint knife counts the new steps as done.
- Pottery shards: the potted plants of Levels 4 and 5 break for them. Two shards pounded with a stone make ceramic grit, and two grit and a block of soil make a blue clay: about four plants a clay, a slow way to clay without the trip to Level 3.
- New world setting *Backrooms map* (under Survival challenges), for players who want to feel lost: *Seen areas* (the map as before, the default), *Seen areas, no waypoints* (the map fills in but marks nothing for you) or *None* (no map or minimap at all, and a little more chalk). Without waypoints, notes and the things that marked the map tell you the direction and paces instead.
- Without waypoints, vanilla's "You died here" waypoint is off too; the First Steps note step and The End's computers are worded for it (the way out told, not marked); Kitty's computer and Level 3999's attendants speak before the way is told; and item texts, the handbook and a few notes no longer promise a map mark.
- Furniture and clutter of the later levels give what is in them again, where breaking them used to give the sticks, rubble or brackets of the wall they stand against: Level 188's minibars (juice, Almond Water, crackers), carts, wardrobes and beds; Level 3999's bins, prize cases, lost and found, dead cabinets and ticket piles; Level Fun's cake tins, gifts and carnivorous balloons; The End's desks, card catalogues, shelves and furniture; Kitty's furniture and plush heaps; the Hub's props; wanderers' loose notes. Scarcity scales the food among it too. Level 3999's plush and neon-lamp prizes and rubble masonry come back whole when broken, instead of breaking down like the building.

## 0.512.2019 — first public release

The first release on the Vintage Story mod database. Everything in the [README](README.md) and the [player guide](docs/GUIDE.md) is in it:

- Twenty-two levels: Levels 0 to 11, the Snackrooms, Level 37 (the Poolrooms), Level 94 (Motion), Level !, Level Fun =), The End, The Hub, Level 974 (Kitty's House), Level 3999 (the Neon Paradise) and Level 188 (the Courtyard of Windows).
- More than seventy kinds of threshold between them, each always leading to the same place and saved with the world.
- Scavenger survival on Vintage Story's own systems, with Level 0 as the only place you wake up.
- The Backrooms' creatures, each with its own behaviour and counters, run by a director that keeps them quiet in the heart of a level and dangerous near its edges.
- A map that shows only what you have seen, wanderers' notes and a journal, a handbook rewritten for the Backrooms, and a First Steps tutorial.
- World settings for creature activity (*Backrooms entities*) and resource scarcity (*Backrooms scarcity*).
- A build check that warns in chat when a server and a player have different copies of the mod.

Worlds are created at layout version 6.
