# Changelog

## 0.701.2 — a light in the deep
- Level 7's ways out are easier to find. A wanderer's note about the ring of standing stones or the cave mouth in the deep now marks the nearest one on your map, in every world. New worlds only (layout version 17): the tops of a stone circle's standing stones glow faintly, a cave mouth is ringed with faintly glowing rock with a glow at the bottom of its shaft, and the rope ladder down to a sunken room hangs beside the room's tunnel instead of in front of it. The handbook's Level 7 page says where to look: both lie shallower than the tar of the abyss.
- The present left for you after escaping Level ! can be opened: break it for its 25 rusty gears and 2 temporal gears. Before, it could not be broken at all.
- Giant Spiders come out for noise. Off the 9th Road, a racket (rock broken with a pickaxe, a fight, a forced door) wakes any spider within 40 blocks, and now and then calls one out of the dark to hunt whoever made it, lamp or no lamp. A fire, water or the road still keep them off.
- A Reality Shift no longer blacks out the levels lit by a sky (Level 10, Level 94, Level 52, Level 73 and Level 47): the lamps fail, the sky does not, and creatures there still see by it. Stability drains everywhere as before.
- Party Fever shows on screen: a countdown while a Partygoer's bite can still be bandaged, then the fever while you have it.
- The pry bar takes off a door set in solid wall too, and shows you the wall behind it (no bracket), so a fake door is no longer a mystery. A door someone's way back is written onto is put back when they arrive, if it was pried away.
- Murky water boils clean by the batch: pour up to six litres into a clay cooking pot on a lit firepit, let it cook, and pour it out again. Salt water still never boils fresh.
- Fire salt that has burnt out leaves a pinch of Fire Salt Ash, which works as flux at the anvil, like powdered borax.
- A dead Deathmoth falls to the ground instead of drifting up to the ceiling.
- Clearer messages: Level 94's painted throne-room doors say that one of the four opens each day, the Hub's locked doors say that only their own level's key opens them, and the handbook says crops need warmth (the Hub is too cold for them).

## 0.701.0 — nine new levels
- Nine new places, each with ways in and out, a handbook page that opens once you have been there, wanderers' notes, its own sounds and its own dangers: Level 73 "The Redlands" (a red plain under a black sky, a castle with the moon over it, and Coran, who leads you out), the Basement (a cellar of furnace rooms, rec rooms and flooded dead ends), Level 52 "School Halls" (a green campus of classrooms, canteens, courts and a gym), Level 31 "Roller Rink" (a rink with a DJ booth, an arcade, a rental counter and a food court), Level 120 "Broken Servos" (a ripped-apart data hall of racks and consoles), Level 15 "Futuristic Halls" (white halls, labs and a door that does not open), Level 23 "The Petrified Garden" (a forest of stone trees under a lid with holes in it, and Base Seedling), Level 14 "The Hospital" (long dark wards, and the Hunter) and Level 47 "The Adderwood" (an old-growth forest with a manor and a wanderer's camp).
- New ways between levels, each always leading to the same place, with the way back built where you arrive: a crack in the floor of Levels 0, 1, 2, 4 and 5 (a fall into the Basement); a wooden door with faint pop music behind it in Levels 2, 3 and 6 (Level 31); a door marked SERVER ROOM and an EMERGENCY EXIT door in Level 4 (Levels 120 and 52) and a high school in the city (Level 52); the white wall at the edge of a Level 10 field and a door in Level 3 (Level 15); a hollow green tree in the Poolrooms (Level 23); the Gloom in Level 9 and Level 6's deepest dark (Level 73); and, very rarely, an EMERGENCY EXIT door in Level 0 (Level 14) and the dead trees of Level 10 (Level 47). Every new level has ways out too: revolving doors and a pair of stolen skates in Level 31, a pane, stairs, a gateway and a MEDICAL CENTER door in Level 15, a collapsing floor and a futuristic door in Level 14, hollows and giants in Level 47, Coran, and the Basement's climb.
- The ways from a level into a later one are a quarter as common in the new levels as they were built, and the ways into a higher class have their M.E.G. placard, as in the older levels. Worlds from every earlier version get the nine levels and their ways in the parts nobody has reached yet.
- Food is under pressure. Torn supply boxes and emptied crates give a Food Scrap (satiety 70) a quarter of the time, and now and then a bit of rot. New worlds only (layout version 15): the food props of Levels 1 to 5 (metal shelving, drums, cardboard boxes, fridges, cupboards, pantry shelves) are picked over like the boxes and found stripped and giving no food: emptied shelving, open drums, flattened cardboard, a fridge door ajar. The Basement's and Level 52's are found stripped in every world.
- Food and drink go further in company: a store broken open with others within 48 blocks in the same level gives 1.5 times its food and drink for two, twice for three, and half as much again for each more.
- Sprinting tires you four times as fast as walking.
- Hounds in Levels 8 and 9 come about half as often, one pack at a time, with a quiet spell of three to five minutes after each (shorter on the busier settings and for a party); a level whose cap is full sends none.
- The way back from the Suburbs to the city works again, in every world, and the pry bar no longer destroys a working door. New worlds only (layout version 16): one city rowhouse front door in four leads to the Suburbs, marked by a red door and a lit lamp, and the rest are locked city doors; one Level 10 barn in five has the wall that is not quite there, and you can see, and at night hear, which.
- Carpet is worth cutting. Twine costs 18 carpet fibres. Crouched, a blade cuts one whole carpet square from fresh carpet and leaves the bare floor. From squares you make a carpet cap, jerkin and leggings (armour like a leather jerkin, and silent), a bedroll and a four-slot carpet bag (4 squares and 2 fibres). The item and wanderer texts now say 18 fibres and squares, not the old numbers.
- Pleather is gone: seats give cloth, and any pleather you carry or have stored turns into carpet squares, and pleather armour into carpet armour. Wanderers no longer sell gambeson.
- Metal armour is loud: chain, lamellar, brigandine and scale carry a runner's or jumper's noise about 1.4 times as far, plate twice as far, and walking in them clanks.
- Things on the walls work, and everything that works makes a noise that hunters follow. Fire alarms ring for 25 seconds and the sprinklers within 12 blocks put out fires, torches and candles under them; steam valves blast scalding steam from the nearest pipe; breakers put out every working light within 14 blocks until you fit a fuse or a day passes (tripped breakers are kept with the world); car alarms, radios and wind-up alarm clocks (now and then in nightstands and dressers) draw whatever hears them. The alarm clock's ring wakes you without sending anything after you.
- Vanilla drinks fill the water bar, and water in a bowl or bucket keeps what it was: tainted, salt or foul water is still that when you drink it from a vessel. Butter is replaced by cheese and Almond Milk in iceboxes and Kitty's fridge, washers give borax, and a Faceling stares before it shoves. New worlds only (layout version 16): Level 8 holds more borax.
- Chalk comes in red, blue, yellow and green (Level 4 filing cabinets, Level 5 suitcases, Level 52 chalkboards), and its marks are fully opaque.
- New clothes, found in every world: t-shirts, jeans, a hoodie, a work jacket, sneakers, work boots and a cap (a little warmth), a lab coat (Level 14 and 15 lockers, Level 120 shelves) and a rare hazmat suit and hood (three quarters off Level 14's contamination poison and stability drain, and the hood slows the Basement's smell of the dead; hot and a little slow, no armour, wears out).
- Items have real models: 81 of the 82 items that were flat sprites, and Almond Water and its bottles, canned beans, crackers, the Lobby's salvage and tools, notes, cards and the levels' own food were remade.
- The new levels are balanced: the Basement's food a quarter, its workshop tools about a thirteenth and seed sacks a seventh; Level 14's caches pay the Level 4 to 6 pool; Level 15's seats and lockers give carpet fibre, not twine; Level 120's racks half the wire and boards; Level 31's server every 12 game hours with Almond Water 30 %; Level 52's snack machines never restock and it has a fifth as many trees; Level 73's table feeds each player once per 12 game hours and Coran leaves no carcass; Level 23's amphorae a third of the clay and its water clean only in the Heart's pool and Base Seedling; Level 47's moose follow the food setting.
- Level 73's ditch before the gate is one block deep everywhere, and the red plain is a shade brighter, so the far fields and a random arrival read better (new worlds only for the ditch, layout version 16).
- The Basement: the cold-beer home bar is in about a quarter of the rec rooms, not seven in ten; the DO NOT ENTER board is on about one swollen-shut door in three, and the rest are bare; and one crack in three has a wooden ladder from the floor to the top of the shaft, which climbs out into Level 0, with a new wanderer's note, "A long ladder" (new worlds only, layout version 16).
- Level 31 has one back arcade per rink complex, not one in every other store room; the other store rooms are repair-bench rooms again. New worlds only (layout version 16).
- Level 120: more racks stand doorless, and only one door in four on the way back to the offices has a lit EXIT sign. New worlds only (layout version 16).
- Level 23's sunbeams fall only over the Heart of Water and the wide glades, not under every hole in the lid. New worlds only (layout version 16).
- Level 14's Hunter has wet skin: a cold painted catch-light along the tops of its body and down its tentacles, a very dark grey base instead of flat black, and a wet rim round its red mouths, so it has a shape in torchlight. Nothing on it glows.
- Level 47: a wanderer's camp ring is now cold in three camps in four (a Cold Fire Ring); only one camp in four is alight (new worlds only, layout version 16). The Great Adder's strike scales with the Danger setting, 12 at the default, at most 14, and no longer a flat 16; one strike still kills the unarmoured.
- The Robo-Man rebuilt (Level 15's dark squares and robot docks, Level 94's castle): disc joints with brass hubs at the shoulders, elbows, hips and knees (the knees now bend as it walks), pressed tin plates with panel lines and rivets, a chest plate with two dials and two lamps, a smoked visor under a brow with two softly glowing amber lenses, pincer hands, rubber-soled feet, and a wind-up key whose bow is two open loops. A Robo-Man standing in an unpowered dock no longer has faintly glowing eyes.
- The Spanish handbook pages (the level and ways pages and the bridge texts) are retranslated from the current English, and a check now fails when a page's Spanish loses its discovery markers.
- The handbook, the notes and the guide cover all of it, and the Spanish text is complete (machine-made, for a native reader to review).
- Performance: Level 47 lays out ten times faster, and Level 73's dark patches are found on a worker thread instead of holding a chunk up.
- Falls and ways: the fall grace now lasts through the long drops (the Basement's crack, Level 14's collapsing floor), so a hard landing is not punished twice; the Basement's looping stair has no turn a sprinter can slip past; Level 14's EMERGENCY EXIT in Level 0 is kept in a Lobby where the roll would have drawn none; and the locked surgery doors of Level 14 can be pried open like the others.
- Map: Level 73 shows on the map (the hills, the path and the castle), its dark heartbeat checks the light at your eyes as the server does, and the map marks of the Basement, Level 120, Level 23 and Level 47 are no longer grey or black.
- People and places: Base Seedling has a quartermaster, a wanderer who trades for what the garden gives; a Level 23 sentry screams once, and only when you face it; the Level 31 server's leftovers for a full pack no longer count as litter, the Coach stops offering skates once you have a pair, and the waypoint's title is in your language; in the Adderwood a shuck's growl and sniffing stop when it stops stalking, the keeper warns each trespasser once; in Level 120 a Misglitch keeps its height. New worlds only (layout version 16): the chalkboards at the ends of Level 52's classrooms are no longer broken, and no arrival in the Adderwood is put on a root.
- For developers: the gate pins layout versions 1 to 15 and checks the hashes twice, once without the new levels' ways in against main's grids and once with them against the branch's own; it also runs the new levels' links modes, every level's validation (0 to 30) and `tools/itemcheck.py`; the links modes of Levels 31 and 47 are tests; `WorldLayout.ExpVersion` reads the new levels' balance as layout 16 in every world; `tools/expclash.py` checks each level's reserved values; mapgen has modes for each new level (`<tag>stats`, `<tag>links`, `showcase`, `featurecheck`, `gencount`, `orphans`); the client rig has `shots.py`, `itemsheet.py` and more scenarios; the aitest harness has the `props` and `bal` groups and its scripts exit non-zero on a failed verdict; and every generator reproduces its committed assets again (the Basement's three sound files aside), `tools/gen_scraps.py` being retired.

## 0.611.0 — the review
- Two players dying close together no longer lose each other's items to the wrong corpse, and a vending machine or shelf nobody has used no longer rolls its stock again every time its part of the level loads (on Sparse or Scarce an empty one could be re-rolled by walking away and back).
- Tar burns again after a server restart, and Faceling moods no longer come back stale after one.
- The Mangled's fog is cleared when you log in, instead of lingering from the last session.
- Level 94's soft landing and the fall grace after a threshold no longer carry over wrongly from an earlier session, so a fall is neither forgiven for no reason nor punished on arrival; nightmares that load again are removed.
- Breaking a wrecked car no longer takes a car parked against it. Level 188's moss tufts break like moss, not like concrete; a death to one of its arms names the arm; breaking free of an arm keeps the glass quiet for a few seconds instead of a fresh arm reaching out at once; a window that straddles two chunks no longer changes half-way; and its dispensers make their sound again.
- Kitty no longer appears and stands in the house for a day when a call was made just before the house was offended; the End terminal no longer sticks in its on, static or party state; the Hub tower's rule leaves creative and spectator players alone; a Level ! lane claim is released only by the player who made it.
- Thirst has a hint now: a chat line the first time the water bar falls under two-fifths, telling you to drink Almond Water or crouch with empty hands and hold right-click on clean water. The bar is called Water everywhere, and its hover reads "Water 85%" instead of a raw number.
- The tutorial reads better: step 1 no longer tells you not to sprint while waiting for you to sprint ("Tap it once to feel the pace, then stop"), step 3 no longer counts the pocket note as a supply box, and step 12 says the right amount (the torch needs two dry grass, so cut four strips), and a later join while the tutorial is unfinished gets a one-line reminder of where to find it.
- Green Almond Water's description now says what it does in minutes: it keeps the tiredness off for about twenty minutes, and about sixteen minutes of it arrives at once when it wears off.
- Level 3999: the arcade says "You won 3 tickets." (not "3 tickets."), tells you to hold to the end of a game when you let go early, and the attendant, the Level 974 food, computer and dresser blocks show a hint on hover.
- The F7 cheat menu and the F8 mark tell players without the controlserver privilege so, instead of opening or failing; the F7 key is named a developer cheat menu with spoilers in the Controls list.
- The world settings say what is the default and what can be changed later: Thirst and Fatigue are on by default and can be switched with `/worldconfig`, and Menace, Scarcity, Map and Level size name their default option.
- /backrooms find uses the same searches as /backrooms feature; /backrooms stairscan is capped at a radius of 128 and 24 sites.
- A way back (the door or elevator built where you arrive) is built even when its wall lies over a chunk border, where before it was sometimes left out with no thin spot either; the Tower's doors fade and face you like every other threshold; a slow arrival can no longer be triggered twice.
- Two players sleeping in the same camp no longer each get their own night visitor, and a visitor that needs the dark is not sent to someone sleeping by a lamp; the Beast and Mangled rolls count a party the way the ordinary rolls do; a Flickering survives a relog on both sides; a creature sent for a player who has since left is not kept coming.
- Multiplayer and language: level names and the way markers appear in your own language, the notes chat uses your own language, Suburb doors respect land claims, and a server tells joining players it keeps a play log (positions, deaths, trades; kept on the host and nothing is sent anywhere): only an operator can switch it off with `/backrooms telemetry off`.
- New worlds only (layout version 14): Level 7's concrete ledge no longer stops square where another level's wall ends at a boundary. The Level 6.1 food court's plant no longer stands in front of a store's only doorway. The passage past a road break in Level 8 no longer has a two-block step at a sharp kink, spider-nest webs and danger no longer cut off at the edge of a neighbouring den, and one-tile pockets enclosed by props on a cell edge are sealed. A gate's breakthrough now stops at one tile of a multi-tile prop (a car, a bed, a tram) instead of clearing it and leaving the rest of the prop broken, and the folded note beside the cache is not put in Level 8 spider nests, which have no cache.
- New worlds only (layout version 14), the Suburbs: the kitchen of the garage-plan houses is no longer split by a full row of furniture, so the stove and fridge can be reached (and the doll houses are no longer cut in two); the stove no longer stands in the back doorway; every house bathroom keeps its toilet and has no enclosed floor tiles; the laundry and medicine cabinet no longer wall off the back of the room; field lots without a gate are fenced right across the back; street signs and lamps are not put on the town's edge walls. Level 11: rubbish bags on the avenue sidewalk no longer stand in front of a corner building's side door.
- New worlds only (layout version 14), the far levels: in Level Fun the service-stair rooms no longer have their only door opening onto a raised step; in the Hub the roundabout around the Tower no longer has blobs of solid wall across the road at its diagonals in worlds of certain map sizes; Level 37's red corridor EXIT sign faces the right way in corridors that run west to east and the far half of a lit gallery across a block line is no longer wrongly dark; in Level ! the privacy curtain is four blocks tall, with its top part, the toppling shelf and swing doors no longer have a hole in the floor opened in front of them, and the three straights roll different obstacle sets.
- New worlds only (layout version 14), the outdoors: pines on Level 10 keep their tips; deep Level 8 has no unlit glowworms and glowcaps below the lit depth; the Level 94 water tower is the same throughout a world.
- Performance: Level 8's caves generate faster, and the Partygoers and the Death Rats think more cheaply, which helps when many are around.
- For developers: `tools/locked.sh` no longer stalls a build; the gate pins every released layout version (1, 9, 10, 11, 12 and 13) and fails on a changed layout hash, reports how many harness cases it skipped and fails when more than three are; and the stairs harness walks a player-sized box up Level 1's stairs to nowhere and Level 5's grand staircase and fails any flight with under two blocks of headroom.

## 0.610.5 — what you have seen
- The handbook learns as you do. "What Lives Here" names only the creatures of the levels you have been to, and opens a page on one only once you have met it: looked at it for a second within 32 blocks, or been hurt by it, or hurt it. "The Levels" lists Levels 0 to 11 by name from the start and opens each one's page once you have stood in it; the levels beyond the rings appear as you reach them. The other pages (Progression, Food and Water, Reality Stability, Salvage, Home, Rest, Finding the Way) keep their advice but name a far level or a creature only once you have found it, and the Starter Guide only hints at what follows runners in Level 0 until you have seen it. The Progression Guide keeps the class ladder, since the M.E.G. placards say the same at every gate. A player on a world begun before 0.610.5 keeps the levels they have visited; the creatures start unmet. For testers, `/backrooms discover all` opens everything and `reset` closes it again.
- Long handbook pages no longer show black on Linux. The game draws a page as one picture as tall as the page, and "What Lives Here" was taller than many graphics drivers allow, so the page came out black with only its links visible. Our pages are drawn in slices now, on every system.
- The F7 cheat menu's Travel tab has a "Go to feature (in this level)" list: the special places of the level you are standing in (Level 0's Red Rooms and Manila Room, the Snackrooms, Level 5's front hall, Level Fun's party rooms and so on, 196 in all), and the teleport turns you to face the place. "Go to nearest" keeps the gate, way out, unopened cache, refuge and den. The Player tab lost its corpse and light buttons and gained "Where am I?"; its headings read "Set state" and "Time awake". The World tab's notes no longer overlap.
- For developers: `GATE_TAG` picks the gate's build folder, so gates run from different branches no longer share one.

## 0.610.4 — pine and paper
- Resin has a source. Nothing in the Backrooms gave it, which stalled axles, gears and windmills, the churn, the fruit press, lamellar armour and glue. Level 10 now grows a few pines among its oaks, and one or two logs low on each trunk are streaked with resin: right-click one for resin, and it oozes again in about a week, as in vanilla. Fell the tree and the resin goes with it, and a pine grown from a seed bleeds none (vanilla's rule too). The pines come with new worlds only (layout version 13): in a world begun before 0.610.4, Level 10 keeps the trees it would have had.
- The crate-pickers of the Level 1 camp and the M.E.G. camp on the 9th Road sell resin, two for 2 rusty gears, a few a week, in old worlds too (from their next weekly restock).
- Schematics from other mods turn up in deep caches. Mods such as BetterRuins hide their schematics in vanilla's ruins, which a Backrooms world never has, so they could never be found. When such a mod is loaded, a box, crate, cupboard or fridge broken open from Level 4 down now and then holds one of its schematics (about one cache in 25, one in 17 from Level 7 down), at the mod's own relative odds; the rarer ones (BetterRuins' mechanical and Jonas schematics) only from Level 7 down. Only schematics and blueprints are taken, never the other things those mods add to ruins (maps to ruins that are not here, metal, gears). Without such a mod, nothing changes.
- Stairs you could not walk up: Level 1's stairs to nowhere and Level 5's grand staircases had the ceiling too low over a step, so a player bumped their head and stopped short of the top. The ceiling is now open far enough to climb them all the way, in new worlds (layout version 13); in older worlds those stairs stay as they were (break the block over the step to get by).
- The handbook's Progression Guide has a Resin section, and the mechanical power, tree and Level 10 pages say where it comes from.

## 0.610.3 — the colour of the way
- Map waypoints for the ways between levels are coloured by where they lead: every way to Level 2 is one colour, every way to Level 3 another, and so on for each level (a gate is the colour of the level on its far side, a Hub door the colour of its level). A way that only takes you to another part of the same level (stairs in Level 1, say) is a grey ladder, so it no longer crowds out the ways that matter. Waypoints you already have keep their pale violet.
- Wanderers buy a few more of the things that had little use: the Level 1 camp takes rotten wood for its barrel, the city's vendor, the Hub's doorstep and Level 3999's campers take Level 3999's circuit boards, the campers its coin mechanisms too, and The End's Junkie buys cat and bunny plush toys, from home. Prices are low, one or two rusty gears.
- Multiplayer: when the lights go out in Level 1 (or the rats' Screaming darkens Level 2), they go out for everyone in the party, not only the player the Hounds were sent for. A friend a room away used to see the lights still burning while the same Hounds came for both of you. The Hounds still come once for the party, and the next lights-out waits as long for each of you.
- The hotel wall panel's recipe no longer clashes with vanilla's wooden fence: it was the same planks and sticks in the same pattern, so the grid could give a panel when you wanted a fence. It is now two planks and a stick in each row (PPS over PPS), the same cost as before.
- The Progression Guide says how to build a pit kiln without digging: it needs no hole, only solid blocks that do not burn under it and on its four sides, so set one in a corner and close it in with two blocks of soil from the dead potted plants.

## 0.610.2 — side by side
- Multiplayer: two or more players together are no longer hunted twice as often. The director used to roll for new arrivals once for each group of players within 24 blocks of each other, so two players only a room or two apart (25 to 64 blocks) each got their own full rate, and the Flickering, the Screaming, the Beast and the Mangled were rolled for every player on their own. Players within 48 blocks of each other (in the same level) now share one budget of arrivals, rolled at 25% more per extra player (two together feel 1.25 times what one does, not 2), and each arrival is aimed at one of them at random; players far apart keep their own. The wanderers' timers are shared the same way. Playing alone is unchanged.
- Hotbar tooltips work again in a Backrooms world: hovering an item in the hotbar shows its name and description, and H opens its handbook page. The thirst and rest bars' frame lies over the hotbar and was taking the mouse's hover before the hotbar saw it; it no longer takes any mouse input (the bars still show their value on hover).

## 0.610.1 — through the gaps
- Partygoers and Hounds now reliably fit through gaps one block wide. The game's pathfinder sets a creature's body down at a random spot across each block it plans through, so a body 0.8 or 0.9 wide fitted a one-wide doorway or tunnel only about half the time, and a Partygoer could still be stuck in its party room and a Hound balked at Level 2's service tunnels. Both bodies are 0.58 wide now; their models and what you can hit are unchanged.

## 0.610.0 — the creatures, the needs and the climb
- Level Fun's Partygoers can leave their party rooms now. They stood 2.2 blocks tall and the rooms' doorways are 2 clear, so the game's pathfinder found no way out for them at all and they could only stand at the head table; they are 1.95 tall now (the model is unchanged).
- Names: the dead player's body and bones entities, and the cheat-menu and mark-moment hotkeys, had no lang name (shown as raw codes in places); they have English and Spanish names now, and a gate check (tools/langcheck.py) keeps every block, item, entity, setting, chat key and handbook page named.
- Saplings grow into trees now. Planted tree seeds went to saplings and then stood forever: the game only loads its tree shapes for its own world types, so the Backrooms had none to grow them with. Seeds and saplings planted on the open levels become oak, birch, maple and the rest, wherever there is room for the tree.
- The wanderers who trade belong where they stand. Each keeps to a site with an identity from the lore: crate-pickers round a barrel in Level 1, an M.E.G. supply outpost in Level 4, an M.E.G. camp on the 9th Road stocked by B.N.T.G. caravans, a B.N.T.G. street vendor in the city, the Junkies of The End, the M.E.G.'s doorstep and Camp Amber in the Hub, and the campers of Level 3999. Each sells what its place has and buys what it lacks, with Almond Water kept scarce (a few bottles a week). They have a dialogue of their own instead of vanilla's ("You just noclip in?"), with useful talk: what lives there and how to deal with it, water and food, safe sleep, the ways out, a rumour of the next level, and whether there is a way home. The Junkies talk to anyone but trade only with someone carrying Memory Juice. Rusty gears stay the coin. Wanderers already in a world take their site's identity and wares the first time they load (their gears and names are kept).
- Hounds fit through gaps one block wide now (their body was 1.1 wide, so they could not get along Level 2's one-wide service tunnels or past clutter in Level 4's cubicles and the hotel's halls): body 0.9 wide, same look, same size to hit. They still slow down for obstacles: through anything under two blocks wide a Hound goes at about 60 % of its speed.
- Thirst: a water bar above the food bar. It runs down about as hunger does (a full one lasts about 25 minutes of walking), faster in the heat, you slow below two-fifths, and empty it costs a little health and stability. Almond Water and the other drinks fill it; world water can be drunk by crouching with empty hands, by its kind (clean pools, puddles that sometimes sicken you, the salt sea). Handbook: Food and Water.
- Fatigue and sleep: a Rest bar above health, about 48 minutes of play awake when full, then drowsy (you can sleep), exhausted and dead on your feet at about two hours, each costing Reality Stability, walking speed and the attention of the things that live here. Sleeping refills it and runs the clock forward while everyone sleeps; pain, something close, or thirst or hunger wake you, and a night's sleep outside a safe place can draw a hunting creature to your bed, halved by a shut door nearby. Handbook: Rest and Sleep.
- Beds everywhere: every bed-like block (hotel, hospital and Kitty's beds, cots, sleeping bags, bedrolls and makeshift beds) can be slept in, each with its own quality for how well it rests you; the foot of a two-part bed works as its head. The new carpet bedroll (16 carpet fibres + 2 flax twine + 4 wallpaper strips) is a craftable bed that goes down anywhere.
- Blue and Green Almond Water: Blue hydrates, steadies you more than Almond Water and makes you drowsy at once; Green hydrates and holds the tiredness off for about twenty minutes, and then the sleep it held back lands all at once.
- Bottles and boiling: drinking a bottled drink leaves an empty bottle (16 to a stack); right-click any water with one to fill it, clean, murky or salt by where it came from. Water coolers, drinking fountains, pumps, taps and seeps no longer produce a bottle from nothing: hold right-click to drink straight from one, or fill an empty bottle at it (each costs one of its limited portions); vending machines and fridges still hand out sealed bottles. ## 0.514.0 — the graphics pass

Nothing vanilla changed, no block or item codes changed, and existing worlds keep working: new looks apply to every block already placed, and the few props with new shapes (Level 9's roofs and gables, Level 94's water towers) appear in chunks generated from now on.

Where no plain stretch of wall is near enough (the middle of a car park or an open office floor, the sea, the dark, the open levels, the city's streets, the far places), and for stairs, vents, pits and every other way, the thin spot is as before. It works in every world, the first time anyone goes through such a door; thin spots already left stay as they are. Level 1's steel doors and Level 4's office doors now say they are locked when tried.
- The Beast of Level 5 lives in the Terror Hotel: rare, and met most often deep in its boiler halls. When he chooses you, the hotel's music stops, a drum beats somewhere far off, something whispers, metal scrapes closer through the pipes, and a gentleman may watch you from the corner of your eye. Leave the hotel or reach the Beverly Room and he lets you be. Otherwise he steps out of a column of flame in a flash of violet light, an octopus-headed gentleman in a black fur suit, and offers a deal. Shake his hand (right-click) and you are healed, fed and steady again, but in three days he comes to collect, wherever you are. Hold his gaze for five seconds without running and he is startled and gone. Refuse him (strike him, walk away, or let the offer lapse) and his coat opens into moth wings: he hunts you with blades for claws, nearly as fast as a sprint. Keep out of his reach for twenty seconds or get somewhere safe; bring him down and he leaves Level 5's Level Key. For admins: `/backrooms beast` (and an L5 Beast button in the cheats menu).
- Level 8 has Giant Spiders, as the lore has it ("Level 8 has a bit of a spider problem"). One as long as you are lies low in the dark off the 9th Road, its eyes shining in your light, its mouthparts clicking now and then. Come within a few blocks of it, run, or make a racket within earshot and it rears up with a hiss and gives chase, faster than you can sprint, with a venomous bite that keeps burning for a few seconds after. It is territorial, not tireless: it lets you go once you are well clear of its ground, stand by a fire, or wade into water (spiders can't swim), and goes back to wait, mending slowly. Creep past to get closer unnoticed. Badly hurt, it flees; killed, it carries a bounty of rusty gears. It climbs the cave walls. In worlds made from now on (layout version 9) the caves also hold spider nests well away from the road: stretches strung with cobwebs, thickest at the heart, with the remains of what was caught lying in them. Inside one it is as dangerous as the edge of the level, and the spiders are what comes. Cobwebs slow you to a crawl but not the spider; they burn.
