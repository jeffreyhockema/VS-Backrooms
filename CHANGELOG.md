# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Level 94 (new worlds): the houses on the hills and in the towns have the Suburbs' real outsides: tall windows in every room's wall (a picture window in the living room, upstairs windows over them, shutters on some), front and back doors that open, and a brick chimney on every house, standing outside a gable end or rising through the ridge, instead of a slit of a window, a doorway with no door and a chimney flat in the wall. The doll's house and flooded house doors are unchanged.
- The End: the labels on the wrapped stock no longer flicker, and the old computers' buzz (the three on the central desk most of all) is a low hum now, not a painful whine.
- Level Fun: the Partygoers fit through the doorways (a little smaller), so they can leave a room; the balloon arches stay under the ceiling and no longer flicker; the lure balloon hangs on one continuous string.
- Level 5: the turntable is a mahogany phonograph with a record, brass tonearm and an open velvet-lined lid, and the room service tray a worn silver-plate service with a cloche, coffee, napkin and a rose.
- You come out of a Level 8 vent through a vent. The vents beside the 9th Road used to put you down in a thin spot in Level 2; now a vent grille is set in the foot of a plain tunnel wall near where you arrive, and you stand on the floor in front of it, facing out. Crawl back into it and it leads home, and it stays (worlds already played keep the thin spots they have). One-way vents (the Snackrooms' to the Electrical Station) and The End's vent into open air are unchanged. `/backrooms wayback sim-vent <level> <n>` tries it.
- When the lights go out in Level 1 it is always dangerous now. Within a few seconds you hear the Hounds, far off (only Hounds, never a rat); five to ten seconds later they are put down out of sight, and they keep coming for as long as the dark lasts, even if you keep running and looking about. The Screaming's dark brings hunters too: Hounds in Level 1, Death Rats in Level 2, and it lasts long enough for them to arrive.
- A fed Skin-Stealer keeps to itself, ambling slowly between real places and stopping to mutter instead of walking into walls; but come within a few blocks of it and it turns on you, until you back well off. It never hunts while fed.
- A hungry Skin-Stealer no longer stares and wanders off: after a short stalk it comes for you, sooner the further out you are (seconds at Level 1's outer edge). A glimpse of a hungry one can turn into a stalk.
- Wanderers' notes are rarer from Level 1 on (the play-test found them too plentiful after Level 0): supply boxes, torn boxes, backpacks, tents, camp props, desks and the rest give half as many ordinary notes outside Level 0 (Level 1 gave a note per four cells with every container broken, now one per ten); Level 0 drops exactly what it did, folded notes and loose notes are unchanged, and every track still completes over a normal playthrough. New harness mode `notesupply`.
- The bones are redrawn. The bone pile was five loose boxes and a cube on one flat cream noise texture; it is now a modelled heap of long bones with shadowed knuckles, small ribs, vertebrae and a skull with eye sockets, nose and teeth, lying on one another. The skeleton now lies inside its one block (its legs used to run a block and a half out of it), with skull and jaw, spine, four pairs of arched ribs, pelvis, arms and bent legs. Bone is a dry, matte off-white with grey-brown mottle, fibre streaks and pores, as vanilla's bones are, and the giant ribs share it. Drops, breaking and boxes are as before.
- In new worlds nearly every Level 1 stairs to nowhere goes on in the dark: a doorway is cut in the wall at the top of the third step, the stairs climb through it and two steps more (over the next stall's ceiling, or along inside the wall itself where the wall is a district's edge) to a dead-end landing, and you are taken elsewhere there rather than at the wall. Turn round and you can walk back out.
- A fed Skin-Stealer that turns hungry sheds the clothes it wore while fed, as the hungry ones look.
- Out of Level ! for the first time, a present waits by the door you came out of: brown paper and twine, a tag that says "For getting out", and inside 25 rusty gears and 2 temporal gears. Once per player, ever.
- The door you come out of Level ! by is now a real door of the level you arrive in (a locked one, never a way somewhere, and never one of The End's EXIT doors), turned into a red fire door; with none near, it is set into the wall. It no longer stands against the wall beside another door, and it carries a NO RE-ENTRY sign instead of a huge red "!" (doors already standing in your world get the new face too). `/backrooms runescape [1|4|end] [reset]` tries it.
- Salvage and arcade materials stack further: scrap wire, metal brackets and rotten wood to 64 (were 16), glass shards, carpet fibres, wallpaper strips, ceramic grit and pot shards to 64 (were 32), and Level 3999's circuit boards, neon tubing and coin mechanisms to 64 (were 32, 32 and 16). Stack sizes are not saved, so worlds already made get them at once.
- Tool tiers matter a little in three places, each with a real reason and none needed for anything (no yield goes above one and a half times the usual): a bronze pickaxe gets a quarter more (an iron one half more) out of pipes, cable runs and machinery than a copper one, since a harder edge cuts them into cleaner, longer pieces; a dead light fixture worked loose with a knife, scraper or chisel in hand gives half as many glass shards again as one that is smashed; and a vanilla knife of copper or better strips wallpaper and carpet two to a cut, as the glass shiv and the scraper do (flint and bone knives still tear off one).
- Building with the Backrooms: a base can now be made of the Backrooms' own blocks. 28 new crafting recipes make bare and Level 4 drywall, yellow, manila and cream wallpaper (and their baseboards), Level 0's carpet, the office and hotel carpets, ceiling and office tiles, hotel panelling and floors, concrete with its floor, ceiling, wall and pavement forms, Level 3's brick, glazed tile and cinder block walls, house floorboards and bathroom tiles, from plaster, mortar, rubble, wallpaper strips, carpet fibres, planks, bricks and clay tiles. And desks, tables, chairs, sofas, wardrobes, bookshelves, dressers and cubicle partitions come away whole when broken with a saw, to set down again facing you (any other tool gives their lumber and cloth as before, even for a piece you set down yourself). What you craft comes back whole when you break it: the blocks the recipes make are player-made twins (`built-*`, same look, sounds and shape) that give themselves back, with an axe or bare hands for drywall, wallpaper, carpet, tiles and boards and a pickaxe for concrete and masonry; the world's own walls still break down, and picking up a world block is still salvage. Lights, machines, doors and the fabric that holds levels together stay salvage only. The handbook's Salvage page has the recipes.
- More to build with: 29 more crafting recipes make dotted wallpaper (bare drywall, 2 strips and charcoal) and pale wallpaper (with quicklime), the lobby's hotel carpet (the maroon with fibres), white, blue and brick house siding, flat and sloping roofs from fired shingles, barn boards (grey, and red from red clay), the porch's deck and step, pool, coping and subway tiles, and the Endless City's concrete, curtain-wall glass, brick and limestone walls and plaster wall. Metal furniture (filing cabinets, cafeteria, folding and stacked chairs, folding tables, luggage carts and service trolleys) comes away whole when broken with a hammer in hand, as the wooden pieces do under a saw.
- Tar can be taken: right-click one of the scalding tar pools of Level 7's seabed or Level 8's rock with an empty bucket and it fills with the game's own tar (the pool is not used up, and still scalds anyone standing in it). A litre of tar and 4 rubble in a barrel make 4 asphalt. The handbook says so on the Salvage page and in Levels 7 and 8.
- A Flickering in Level 1 gives you a moment: when the lights fail, the Hounds are heard far off for eight to fifteen seconds before they come, from twenty-odd blocks away and never from where you were just looking; and a Hound the Flickering sent hunts you until the lights come back, searching where it lost you and coming on again, so running only buys time. A shut door, a fire and a steady stare still hold it.
- Your first wanderer's note no longer points back at the boxes beside where you woke (and so marked nothing, since the mark landed on the "where you woke" waypoint): it names a box you have not been to.
- The red EXIT door into Level ! looks like a door from behind too: the push side of a fire door, with a pull handle, a kick plate and a door closer, instead of a blank red slab.
- Four small layout fixes, in worlds made from now on (layout version 11): tents and sleeping bags in the open are placed where they fit instead of being dropped from a wrong search window, Level 0's party cells are never near spawn, every stretch of the hotel's lobby has a front hall (a few at the hotel's rim had none), and Level 3999's rusted cabinets turn up in the whole band round a blackout. Worlds made earlier keep their layouts.
- Level 0's arched areas are redesigned, in new worlds (layout version 10): they are the Lobby's own rooms and halls in plain cream stucco, with some walls made into walls of archways, rows of round arches over half-height walls that you see through into the next room, as in the wiki's photo, and now and then a full-height arch to walk under. They are as easy to get about as the Lobby, lit the same, and hold no more supplies than it does (their supply boxes were about seven times as dense). Worlds made earlier keep the pale paper and their old arched areas.
- Level 0's pale arch paper (the walls and arch headers of the arched areas) is a little less bright, in every world: the arched areas are lit exactly as the rooms around them, but the paper was a fifth brighter than the yellow and looked over-lit. It is now about as bright as the yellow, still paler in hue.
- Level 8's 9th Road is lonelier, in worlds made from now on (layout version 10). Camps are about a quarter as many, so there is no longer a campfire every few hundred blocks, and there are fewer branches and ways off the road. The road is also broken in places: it ends in a wall of rubble (a rockfall, with the lamps stopped short), a hundred blocks or more of solid rock lie beyond, and the road starts again on the far side. The way on is down through the caves, away from the light: a cave mouth opens in the road's wall just before the rubble, and an unlit passage winds off from the road, down and out into the dark, past chambers and side passages that lead nowhere, and back up to the road beyond the break. Worlds made earlier keep the road as it was.
- Sand, so mortar and the refractory bricks for a bloomery are possible: in new worlds the floor of Level 7's sea, shallows and depths, is sand (with outcrops of the islands' rock and a fringe of gravel), the main source and a dive away, and a few of Level 10's ponds have a few blocks of sand on the bed. Worlds made earlier are unchanged.
- Level 94's picket fences sit better on the hills: a house's fenced front yard now stands on a level plot (the hill falls away outside the fence, not along it), the fences round a hill follow the ground evenly, and where the ground still steps by a block the fence is a post either side of the step instead of panels hanging off or buried in the next block. In worlds made from now on; existing worlds keep their fences.
- Vanilla blocks in place of three look-alikes, in worlds made from now on (layout version 10): a camp's lantern is vanilla's small lantern (so it goes in the offhand and picks up with a right-click, without punching it), Level 10's round bales are hay blocks lying on their sides, and its feed troughs are vanilla's small trough. The old blocks stay in worlds already made, and when broken give the vanilla lantern, trough or hay, so nothing is lost; nothing was removed or renamed.
- Selection and collision boxes of furniture and fittings now turn with the model: the Level 4 desk and vending machine, the Level 5 nightstand, sofa, wardrobe, wingchair, boiler and furnace, the hanging EXIT sign and the overhead sprinkler pipe, clearance bar and floor cables used the same box facing every way, so a desk facing east was outlined (and collided) as if it faced north.
- Level 3's manhole tunnels lead somewhere, in worlds made from now on (layout version 10): every cell has a flooded tunnel running its whole width under one of its corridors, and a manhole's tunnel joins it, so the tunnels run on from cell to cell under the level instead of stopping after 9 to 34 blocks. Worlds made earlier keep their dead ends.
- Level 7's entry rooms: the first step of the stairs up from the sea has headroom to climb on, in worlds made from now on (layout version 10).
- The way back from a one-way door or elevator is built more often: in Level 1 it left a thin spot nearly half the time. The search for a place to put it now walks through doorways, reaches further, and accepts a thin partition when no solid wall is near. Existing worlds get this too.
- Red Rooms: the red air now arrives at the wall, not over the first blocks inside.
- Level 1: parked cars are solid and no longer see-through, and the NO ENTRY door plate is redrawn, a red plate with a white bar and bold lettering.
- The End: the plastic wrap is redrawn as a film with sheen streaks and creases, and no longer turns the whole prop translucent; the wrapped chair is a plain chair.
- Fixes from a whole-project code review. Existing worlds get all of these unless noted. Creatures: the Neighborhood Watch no longer drops a charge because someone with a light walks by; the Giant Spider's venom no longer doubles when it bites a second person; a Partygoer pack held off by a fire gives up after twenty seconds; a half-joined player is never attacked; a wanderer is put down out of everyone's sight; only one Mangled comes at a time, and it is rarer on lower creature settings; the Beast returns a day after a reprieve. Levels: The End's isolation clock, Level Fun's Party Fever and Kitty's watch no longer run on while you are logged out; the M.E.G. key stock clears last week's keys and never overwrites a ware; Level 3999's directory gives paces like the catalogue cards; Level 188's arm hurts exactly once a second; chat, errors and waypoint titles come in each player's own language; a waypoint is not added twice for the same place read from another storey; the tutorial still starts by itself after a first visit in creative mode; breaking one part of a tent, sleeping bag or wrecked car no longer removes a part inside someone else's claim; the explored map retries a failed save. In chunks generated from now on, Level 0's pit shafts are carpeted on all four walls (two before), and in worlds made from now on (layout version 10) a Red Room over an arched area is red, no arch is left as half an arch, supply boxes keep off arches, and every Level 8 passage mouth opens onto the road with no passage cut by another. Many systems do less repeated work, so the game runs smoother, and ten blocks that showed a raw name (the hospital's short crash rail, Level 3's pipe joints and brackets) are named.
- The Backrooms are far emptier. In the heart of Levels 0 and 1 a creature turns up about once every twenty minutes or less often, more toward the edges and deeper down; only one kind of thing is about you at a time, and the sounds are far rarer. Level 0 holds only its dark figures, with Smilers only in the Red Rooms and when the lights fail; Level 4 is nearly empty; Facelings are rare, and come one at a time. Hounds are faster than a sprint, and a light no longer slows them. You can still shake one off: get out of its sight (they hunt by sight and hearing, not scent), stare it down and walk calmly away while facing it, or put a shut door between you. Running or turning your back brings it on at once. Existing worlds get all of this: nothing in the layout changed.

## 0.514.0 — the graphics pass

Every texture and model the mod adds was reviewed in the game against the vanilla blocks beside it, level by level, and rebuilt where it fell short. Nothing vanilla changed, no block or item codes changed, and existing worlds keep working: new looks apply to every block already placed, and the few props with new shapes (Level 9's roofs and gables, Level 94's water towers) appear in chunks generated from now on.

In numbers: 1,219 block textures (611 of them new versions, so a surface no longer repeats block by block), 59 creature and character textures, 62 item icons and 608 block models were redrawn or rebuilt across all 22 levels. At the start 180 asset families were graded against vanilla and none matched it; at the end 196 families were compared side by side, before and after, and all but one were kept (13 after a tweak), the exception being Level 2's concrete tunnel walls, which went back to their old look.

- Level ! has fewer hanging EXIT signs on its ceiling: in a new world they run down the hall in a steady line, about a dozen blocks apart, instead of bunching in clusters, with the same sign over every exit and dark stretches only near the end (worlds made earlier keep their hall as it was).
- New world setting, *Backrooms level size* (under World generation): make the rings of levels from half as wide (50%) to twice as wide (200%), so it takes less or more walking to get from one level to the next. The rooms and buildings stay the same size and the far places stay where they are. It is fixed when the world is made; existing worlds stay at 100%, as they were.
- Level 10: livestock now turns up where you would look for it. Hens, sheep, pigs and goats, with lambs, piglets, kids and chicks among them, graze in flocks and herds round the farmsteads and in the paddocks, and strays wander the fallow meadows (vanilla has no cattle). Backrooms scarcity still decides how many there are: three about you on Scarce, nine on Normal, more on Plentiful.
- New creature in Level 9: the Neighborhood Watch, a bloodshot eyeball the size of a man on six legs three blocks tall, walking the Suburbs' streets. It has no ears and no nose and finds you by sight alone: a dozen blocks off in the dark, forty if you carry a light or stand in one. Its faintly glowing eye turns toward whoever it is making out; once sure, it stares and comes faster than you can run, but it only chases what it can see, so break its line of sight and it searches where it lost you (and may wait nearby). It is too tall for doors: indoors you are safe, though it watches through the windows and taps on the glass, which wears on your stability. It can be killed, for a good bounty.
- Level 6.1: the vending machine in the doorway of a vending-machine room is now a broken one (cracked glass, dented panel, a few packets fallen inside, dead display) that you can break up for the snacks it held, so you can get in.
- Surfaces: wallpapers, carpets, concrete, brick, tile, plaster, asphalt, turf and marble are built from a shared material kit with real structure and shading, tile without visible block edges, and no longer repeat the same mark on every block.
- Hardware is geometry, not paint: gauges, dials, grilles, door handles, kick plates, hinges, breaker panels, cable trays, lamps, chandeliers, sconces, elevator doors and call buttons.
- Furniture is modelled: desks, office chairs, filing cabinets, diner booths, store shelves stocked with packs, food-court chairs and tables, arcade cabinets, vending machines, the hospital bed, the Fort Surrender furniture.
- Level 1: poured concrete floors and formed walls, a new camp kit (tent, sleeping bag, burn barrel), framed crates and a looted crate.
- Level 2: machinery cabinets with real dials, the dead tram with recessed windows and a grille, modelled steel doors.
- Level 3: breaker panels, fans, cable trays, risers, a riveted boiler, switchgear and transformer, enamel plaques, the Sanctum's ashlar and statue.
- Level 5: panelled hotel doors with brass plates, an eight-arm chandelier, the front hall desk, key rack and stairs, the cage lift.
- Level 9: gables carry the house's own siding to the roof; pitched shingle roofs with eaves, gutters and ridge caps; porch posts, front doors, mailboxes; fine-grained asphalt.
- Level 11: façade windows, string courses and cornices as geometry; subway tracks, tiles and signs; billboards and advertising pillars with crisp posters.
- Level 11's street and lobby props are rebuilt with real parts: the fire hydrant with its bonnet, nozzles and chains, the mailbox with a domed top, slot and legs, the phone booth with glass panes, a payphone and a directory shelf, the grand piano with keys, lid and pedals, the medical cabinet with doors, handles and a red cross, the pantry shelf with stocked tins and jars, the steel basement door and the lobby's directory board. Every one keeps its place and works as before.
- Levels 37 and 94: shaded mosaic with recessed grout and a modelled POOL door; clay turf without the repeating swirl, the castle, water towers, fountains and milk vans.
- Level 94's water towers are rebuilt as the white municipal tower in the pictures: four braced legs on footings, a riser over the Almond Water tap, a round tank with a conical roof, a catwalk with a railing, ladders, and rust runs on the white paint. They appear in chunks generated from now on.
- Level 94, in worlds made from now on (layout version 8): every house is cream-yellow, as in the pictures (some were white), and the towns' siren poles stand at the corners across the road from the plaza (the house lots used to cover them, so the tune played from nowhere). Existing worlds keep the layout they were made with.
- Level 94's picket fences, in new worlds (layout version 9): they stand back from the road behind a grass verge and go round a hill's own ground, joined at the corners and along winding roads, with a gate where the path from the front door comes down and an opening halfway along each road, so there is always a way in. No more stray posts and one-block gaps. Front yards in the hills and towns are fenced all round, with a gate at the path. Existing worlds keep the layout they were made with.
- The STOP sign (Levels 1, 9 and 94) is a proper octagonal plate: red with a white border and bold white STOP lettering readable from across the road, a plain aluminium back, bolted to a galvanised square post.
- Levels no longer bleed into each other at their edges, in worlds made from now on (layout version 9): each level looks like itself right up to the wall, and the wall shows each side its own face (Level 0's wallpaper on the Lobby side, Level 1's concrete on the car park's), so Level 0 no longer wears to concrete and loses ceiling tiles near Level 1, Level 1 grows no pipes near Level 2, Level 3's cables stay cables up to Level 2's wall, Level 4 no longer turns hotel red near Level 5, and the Snackrooms' mall wall no longer faces Level 6's maze. The only mixing left is in the way through a gate: Level 0's carpet is worn to the concrete in the doorway to Level 1, and Level 4's is the hotel's red in the doorway to Level 5. Existing worlds keep the layout they were made with.

Existing worlds keep the layout they were made with.

An apartment block's lobby leads to a corridor of flats, a few furnished with a bed, a kitchenette, a table for two and a radiator, the rest empty, with plaster ceilings and domed lights. Stores have a stockroom behind the shop. Nothing stands against the plate glass or in a doorway, seats face their tables, and every room's lights are spread evenly over it.
