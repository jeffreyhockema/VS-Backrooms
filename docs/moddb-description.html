Wake up in the Backrooms with nothing.

No tools, no food, no daylight. A folded note in your pocket, two supply boxes beside you, and an endless expanse of mono-yellow rooms under buzzing fluorescent lights. The carpet is damp. The hum never stops. Somewhere down the hall a light is failing.

This is not a maze you escape. It is a world you learn to survive in.

Backrooms: Survival Story is a complete survival game built on Vintage Story's own systems and moved into a building with no outside. Hunger, temporal stability, knapping, pottery, smelting, farming and animal husbandry all still work. What changes is where everything comes from. There are no trees, no rivers, no sunrise. You start by cutting a blade from a glass shard, and you end up with a lit home in Level 0, crops under the ceiling lights, copper from the pipes, iron from the caves, and a map of the ways between levels that you drew yourself.

What's new in 0.514.0

A new look for every level. Every texture and model the mod adds was rebuilt beside the vanilla blocks around it, level by level, so the Backrooms now look like they belong in Vintage Story: wallpaper, carpet, concrete, brick and tile with real structure and shading and no visible block edges; gauges, handles, grilles, lamps and doors made as real shapes instead of paint; desks, chairs, shelves, diner booths, arcade cabinets and vending machines modelled; and every creature and item icon repainted with light and shade. Level 94's houses are all cream-yellow, its siren poles stand at last, and a white municipal water tower rises over each town (in worlds made from now on). Nothing vanilla changed, and existing worlds keep working.

In numbers: 1,219 block textures, 59 creature and character textures, 62 item icons and 608 block models were redrawn or rebuilt across all 22 levels, and 196 asset families were compared side by side, before and after; all but one were kept.

What's new in 0.513.0

The first update, built from the first players' feedback. Existing worlds carry on as they are.

  • Mapless mode. A new world setting, Backrooms map: keep the map as it was, keep it but with nothing marked for you, or have no map at all and find your way by notes and chalk.
  • Scarcer food. A new Scarce setting (about a third of the food). Scarcity now reaches every food and drink, the vending machines and water coolers, Level 10's livestock, and, in new worlds, the fields and berry bushes of Levels 9 and 10.
  • The early tools matter. Flint is now a Level 1 find, so the glass shiv and the improvised scraper carry you through the Lobby, and they cut wallpaper and carpet two strips at a time. Metal brackets are rarer. The First Steps tutorial no longer asks for flint in Level 0.
  • Pottery shards. The potted plants of Levels 4 and 5 break for shards; shards pounded with a stone make ceramic grit, and grit with soil makes clay. Slow, but it saves the trip to Level 3.
  • Everything can be set down. Every item of the mod can be placed on the ground (shift + right-click).
  • Fixes. Scrap wire, circuit boards and coin mechanisms go in a crucible, as the handbook always said. The ladders down Level 3's manholes can be climbed. The furniture of the far levels gives what is in it again.

Surviving

You are a scavenger. Most of what you own is salvage. Supply boxes, crates, furniture, dead light fixtures and ceiling tiles break for scraps. Walls and carpet can be cut for wallpaper strips and carpet fibres. A glass shard is your first blade. With it, the strips and fibres it cuts, and a metal bracket from a dead light, you make a glass shiv, an improvised scraper, twine, a carpet bag and tinder, all from Level 0 alone. The shiv and the scraper cut cleanly, two strips or fibres at a time, where any other knife cuts one.

From there the normal Vintage Story path carries on with materials found level by level: flint in the car park and crates of Level 1, copper and lead in the pipe tunnels, clay in the flooded tunnels under the Electrical Station (or, slowly, from the pottery shards of Level 4's potted plants), coal beside the generators and boilers, real ore veins in the caves, crops and livestock in the farmland.

What you can break, and what you cannot. Drywall, plaster and wallpaper give way to any tool, and give almost nothing. Concrete, brick, tile and every floor slab need a copper pickaxe, like rock, and break into rubble; four rubble make a block of masonry to build with. Pipes, railings and machinery take a pickaxe too and give metal brackets, scrap wire and now and then a copper or lead bit, and the wire melts down in a crucible. Some things never break: each level's roof, the wall between two levels above a certain height, and every door, elevator and vent. Under every floor lie eight blocks of concrete, and under that the ordinary ground Vintage Story lays down in any world, with its strata, caves and ore.
Home is Level 0. Die anywhere and you wake in the Lobby; what you carried stays where you fell. A temporal gear still moves where you wake, but only within Level 0. Everything a home needs has to be brought back from deeper levels: a door, a chest, fire, seeds, soil. Crops grow under the ceiling lights, watered from a flooded room or a bucket.
Food is scarce. Most supply boxes and crates were gone through long ago. A box with its lid torn open or a crate already emptied holds no food, and you can spot one from across the room, so you learn where to look: the deeper the level, the less has been picked over, and the rooms behind locked doors and the hearts of dens were never touched. Hunger runs at 125%. Heat makes it worse, and the cold levels want clothes.
Reality Stability. Vintage Story's temporal stability, with the Backrooms' own geography. Each area has its own stability. Where it is low the gear turns backwards and yours drains; when yours is low the world distorts. Almond Water restores it. Once in every thirty in-game hours or so the lights fail for a few hours in a Reality Shift, and stability drains everywhere.
Finding the way

A map that only shows what you have seen. The map reveals what is in your line of sight, so walls block it, and it remembers between sessions. Every gate you walk through and every elevator, stairwell, door or pit you take is marked the first time, with the way back where there is one. Surveyor's chalk marks walls with arrows, crosses and circles that show on the map.

If that is too much help, the Backrooms map world setting can keep the map but mark nothing for you, or take the map away altogether. Then the notes tell you a direction and a number of paces, and chalk is how you find your way home.

Notes and the journal. Earlier wanderers left notes in boxes, desks, backpacks and bedrolls. They come in order, carrying you from waking to the far levels, and each one points you to the nearest gate, way out, cache or refuge: marked on your map, or told as a direction and paces. Everything you read is kept in your journal. Folded notes, rarer, give exact routes, odds and rules.

The handbook is rewritten. In a Backrooms world the Guides tab is written for the Backrooms: the Starter Guide; the Progression Guide; Reality Stability; Home and Expeditions; Finding the Way; Salvage; Food, Heat and Cold; The Levels, in two pages; Ways Between Levels; and What Lives Here. Every vanilla guide opens with an "In the Backrooms" preface saying what is different here. A fifteen-step First Steps tutorial starts the first time you join.

The topology lies

The levels are laid out in nested regions around where you wake: Level 0 in the middle, Level 1 around it, Level 2 around that, and so on out to Level 10. No region is a circle. A level may end after a few hundred blocks in one direction and run for kilometres in another, and the short way through one level need not line up with the short way through the next. Where two levels meet, the wall is solid but for gates every few hundred blocks. Beyond the rings lie places no amount of walking reaches, only noclipping.

That is only how the world is built. The real map is the network of ways between levels, and it follows two rules so you can learn it:

Rule 1
Every way between levels always leads to the same place. Where it leads is fixed the first time anyone goes through it and saved with the world.
Rule 2
If you walked through it, you can walk back. If you fell, dived or were taken, you cannot. Elevators arrive beside elevators, stairs beside stairs, doors beside doors, and that partner becomes the way back. Where there is no partner, a thin spot is left in the air to step back through. A few doors close behind you for good: the Hub's carved doors, every way out of the Snackrooms but one, the doors into Level !, and a lone doorframe in the Office.

Elevators, stairwells, hotel doors, steel tunnel doors, vents, barn walls that aren't quite there, cinema screens, backyard pools, fountains, manholes, tripwires, hungry vending machines, posters, windows where no window should be: more than seventy different ways tie the levels together, the same way vanilla translocators pair up once repaired. Finding them is a matter of mapping, supplies and luck. Building routes out of what you have found is the game.

Things that live here

What lives in the Backrooms is there to be heard and glimpsed far more than fought. In the heart of a level you hear things down the hall and see things far off that leave when you come near. Further out, things follow at a distance. Near the edge into the next level, and near its elevators, stairs and pits, they hunt.

How bold they come depends on where you are and what you do: the dark, low stability, running, the noise of breaking something open or forcing a door, and how long you have gone without rest all bring them closer. Dens scattered through the dangerous levels are as bad as any edge, and chalk crosses on the walls warn you off. The heart of Level 0 stays empty except during a Reality Shift; further out, things watch and follow. A minute by a fire, beside a wanderer, asleep in a bed, in a safe place or at home counts as rest.

Every creature has a counter you earn as you go; the handbook page What Lives Here has the details. There are also Flickerings out towards the edge of Level 1, the Screaming of the rats, and the horde of Level !. Wanderers who trade keep camps along the way, and nothing hostile closes in beside them. Firesalt, found in coal heaps or made from salt and sulfur, burns for half a day and, like a lit firepit, keeps Hounds, Skin-Stealers, rats, moths, Partygoers and the wooden and paper Animations eight blocks off; its light holds Smilers too.

The levels

The rings

Level 0, the Lobby. The classic: damp Berber carpet, yellow chevron wallpaper, a fissured drop ceiling and lights in a grid. A labyrinth of rooms and hallways that turn, zigzag and open into one another. Pillar halls, arched rooms, open offices, blackout zones, pit rooms, the Manila Room, and the Red Rooms nobody should enter.
Level 1, the Habitable Zone. A car park that never ends: columns, bay letters, abandoned cars, puddles, dead security cameras, stair cores under lit EXIT signs. Maintenance halls of tight corridors, workshops, pump rooms and wanderers' camps.
Level 2, Pipe Dreams. Hot, dark utility tunnels with real 3D pipework overhead and along the walls, hissing joints, steel doors that are mostly locked, and a maze of service tunnels with far too many dead ends.
Level 3, the Electrical Station. Brick and glazed-tile halls crowded with transformers, switchgear, generators and cable trays. Numbered electrical rooms, boiler rooms, flooded tunnels under manholes, and the hushed Sanctums.
Level 4, the Abandoned Office. One quiet office floor: grey carpet with the marks of desks long gone, cubicle farms stripped to partitions, break rooms, server rooms, elevator lobbies, and windows that look out on rain that never ends.
Level 5, the Terror Hotel. A 1930s grand hotel, oddly well kept. Double-loaded corridors of numbered rooms, grand lobbies under chandeliers, dining halls laid for a dinner nobody comes to, the Beverly Room, and boiler halls behind STAFF ONLY doors.
Level 6, Lights Out. Darkness that light barely reaches. Trunk corridors, blind mazes, chalk arrows left by earlier explorers, and the sound of waves from the way the stairs lie.
Level 6.1, the Snackrooms. Rare sealed food courts hidden inside Level 6: bright, safe, and stocked. Getting in is unreliable, and so is getting out, unless you know the right diner in the city.
Level 7, Thalassophobia. An endless sea under a concrete ceiling, deeper and darker the further out you swim. Entry rooms in the ceiling, islands with old camps, kelp, a dead reef, and something that circles divers.
Level 8, Cave Systems. Real Vintage Story geology: strata, ore veins, flooded pockets, stalactites, and strata that are wrong. The lamplit 9th Road runs through it, with camps at its crossings.
Level 9, the Suburbs. A neighbourhood at midnight that never gets light. Houses to search room by room, fenced backyards, cars at the kerb, and foxes and raccoons in the long grass.
Level 10, Bumper Crop. Farmland under an overcast sky: vast fields of spelt, rye and flax, farmsteads with barns and silos, livestock in the paddocks, ponds in the hollows. One of the safest places there is. The soil is thin, though.
Not a ring
Level 11, the Endless City. Fifty kilometres out, behind a wall of black glass that nobody walks through. You noclip in through a barn wall in Level 10. Downtown towers, apartment blocks over stores, rowhouse streets, a subway under every avenue, parks, churches, a museum, and the best-connected set of ways out in the Backrooms.

Beyond the rings

Level 37, Sublimity (the Poolrooms). White mosaic tile and lukewarm water in rotundas, natatoriums, serpentine halls and tunnels of arches, lit by glare from slits you can never see behind. The water heals. Far out lies the Sanguine Reservoir.
Level 94, Motion. A stop-motion picture that never stops being made: striped green hills with one cream house on each, winding roads, water towers of Almond Water, a glass mountain with a castle on top. Safe by day. Be indoors by dusk.
Level !, Run For Your Life! One hospital hallway lit by red EXIT signs, an alarm, and a horde behind you. Sprinting is the only answer.
Level Fun =). An endless party on a ship that cannot be this big. Cake, balloons, bunting, and guests who are not people.
The End. A library that looks like the way out, and is the cruellest trap in the Backrooms.
The Hub. A crossroads of road tunnels lined with carved doors to almost every level. Three open for anyone; the rest want a Level Key, and the pink one wants something cute. Cold, silent and safe.
Level 974, Kitty's House. A small pink house with no outside, where something three metres tall takes you if you carry something cute.
Level 3999, the Neon Paradise. An endless arcade-mall of tickets and prizes, safe at its heart and dark at its fringe.
Level 188, the Courtyard of Windows. A hotel courtyard walled in by 160 windows. Some of them show other places. Some of them beckon.
Getting started
  1. Install the mod as usual. On a server, everyone needs the same build; the mod checks on joining and warns in chat if a client's copy differs.
  2. Create a new world and pick the Backrooms: Survival Story playstyle. Existing worlds and other playstyles are not affected.
  3. Read the folded note in your pocket, open the two boxes beside you, and press H for the handbook. The First Steps tutorial walks you through your first blades, your bag and your first torch.
  4. Keep the map (M) open often. It only knows what you have seen.
