# Wanderers who trade: who they are, what they say, what they deal in

The wanderers who trade used to be one vanilla trader with one trade list and vanilla's dialogue ("You just wake up?"). They now have the Backrooms' lore and the place they keep to. Each wanderer has a **site**, and the site sets three things: the trade list, a name from that site's list (only for wanderers put down from now on), and the site's lines in a dialogue written for the Backrooms. The lore behind all of it, with sources, is in [lore.md](lore.md).

**Status (2026-10-25):** built on branch `pt/traders`. The trade lists, the site keys and the dialogue file were checked on a headless server, and the dialogue by `tools/gen_dialogue.py --check`. Nobody has seen the conversation in a client yet.

## How it works

- **The coin stays rusty gears.** The kill bounty (`src/Entities/Rewards.cs`) pays in gears, and the dialogue explains them in Backrooms terms: nobody makes them and nobody can fake them, so they count. Out here things are worth so many bottles of Almond Water, but water you drink and gears you can only spend. The B.N.T.G. vendor in the city says their own coin is presses, and that outsiders pay in gears.
- **The site key** is a dialogue variable on the entity: `entity.backroomsSite` (the watched `variables` tree, a string), one of `camp`, `outpost`, `road`, `city`, `end`, `hub`, `amber`, `3999`. `WandererSystem` takes it from the level the wanderer stands in. In the Hub it is `hub` within 24 blocks of the M.E.G.'s doorstep by the city door, and `amber` at Camp Amber's spot by the Level 1 door. A wanderer spawned outside these levels (the cheat menu) gets `camp`.
- **The class stays vanilla's `EntityTrader`,** and the codes are unchanged (`backrooms:wanderer-male`). A subclass was tried first, but a saved entity keeps the class name it was saved with (an old wanderer loaded from a copy of a play-test world was still an `EntityTrader`), so only new wanderers would have got it. Everything is done from `WandererSystem` and `src/Entities/WandererSites.cs` instead.
- **The trade lists** are `assets/backrooms/config/trade/wanderer-<site>.json`, written by `tools/gen_entities.py` (`WANDERER_SITES`). Vanilla reads a trader's list from the entity type every time the trader loads, so `WandererSystem` puts the site's list back (`TradeProps`, a public field) whenever a wanderer loads or spawns. The weekly restock then draws from the site's list. The type's own `tradeProps` is the camp's list.
- **New wanderers** get their key before they are spawned and are stocked from their site's list as they spawn. They are named from the site's names (`backroomsSiteNames` in the entity type).
- **Wanderers saved before this update** get their key the first time they load. When that happens their wares are cleared and they are stocked once from the site's list (vanilla's protected `RefreshBuyingSellingInventory`, called by reflection), so the change shows at once. They keep their gears and their names. Their chunk is marked modified so the key is saved: without that, a chunk that is only loaded and unloaded is not written, and the wanderer would be restocked again at every load. Saved data: the only new thing saved is the `backroomsSite` string on the entity.
- **The Junkies' rule is in the dialogue.** The talk option that opens the trade screen has a `player.inventory` condition on Memory Juice (`backrooms:end-memoryjuice`). The game checks it on both client and server, so without juice a Junkie will talk but will not trade. The old `EntityBehaviorJunkie`, which refused any interaction without juice and posted a chat line, is removed. Its lang keys (`endjunkie-*`, from `tools/gen_end.py`) are no longer used.
- **The dialogue** is `assets/backrooms/config/dialogue/wanderer.json`, with its lines in `lang/en.json` (`dialogue-wanderer-*`). Both are written by `tools/gen_dialogue.py`, which also runs the checks: every jump target exists, every line is in en.json, every variable tested is set somewhere, and every site, with and without juice, has a line where one is due and exactly one way to the trade screen. Other languages fall back to English for these keys.
- **Checks in the game:** when a world starts, `WandererSystem` reads every site's list, resolves every ware, parses the dialogue, and logs one line (a warning if anything fails). `/backrooms wanderers [list|load|spawn|adopt <site>]` is a dev test (see `docs/DEVELOPING.md`).

## The sites

Prices are in rusty gears (1 to 8, as before). Stock is per weekly restock (vanilla's `RestockOpts` is never used: stock only comes back with the weekly refresh), and kept small: a site has two to four bottles of Almond Water a week (up to five at the Level 4 outpost), sold one at a time. Each wanderer shows a random selection of up to "shown" of its wares, as vanilla's traders do.

Between them the basics are always somewhere. The camp and the outpost sell Firesalt, bandages and seeds. The camp, outpost, Hub and Camp Amber buy salvage (brackets, wire, glass shards). Bronze knives and spears are on the Road, and knives in the city. The Level 1 notes say Level 1's wanderers trade salvage and hides for Almond Water, Firesalt, candles, bandages and seeds, and the camp's list does exactly that.

Names for new wanderers: camp survivors are named for what they carry (Pell, Barrel, Old Lamp, Stray...). M.E.G. staff go by callsign (Compass, Quartermaster, Survey on Level 4; Lumen, Gravel, Marker on the Road; Doorman, Keyholder, Register at the Hub). B.N.T.G. vendors use trade names (Prospero, Margin, Bargain). The Junkies are Wick, Sunday, Porch Light and Daydream. Camp Amber has Amber, Primer and Signpost, and Level 3999 has Token, Seven, Lobby and Neon.

## The dialogue

About the size of vanilla's trader tree, plus the topics a wanderer would actually talk about: 42 parts, 130 lines (121 lang keys). Lines are short.

- **Meeting.** A wanderer you have not met greets you with its site's line, for example "Easy. You're among people. Come and sit by the barrel." (camp) or "Congratulations, wanderer! You've found an M.E.G. outpost..." (outpost). The Junkie has two greetings: one if you carry juice, and "Got any juice? No? Then you're only passing through." if you don't. Someone who has never talked to a wanderer is then asked "You just noclip in?" and can answer: "Noclip?", "There was a wall, and then there wasn't.", "Is any of this real?", "It's good to see a person." or "What have you got?". After that, wanderers already met say "welcome back".
- **Topics** (the main menu): trade; "Who are you?" (the name, revealed, and the site's identity); "Why rusty gears?" (and why not Almond Water, and where to find more); "What lives around here?" (a shared line, then the site's own creatures and counters, then Smilers, Hounds, Skin-Stealers and Facelings on request); "Where do I find water? Food?" (Almond Water and coolers; the site's water; boiling murky water; Blue and Green); "Is it safe to sleep?" (drowsy first, eat and drink first, nothing visits a sleeper beside a wanderer; the site's advice; the carpet bedroll); "How do I get out of here?" (gates and chalk arrows for the ring levels; the site's real ways out as hints; reading and passing on notes); "Heard anything about what's next?" (one rumour of a nearby level per site); "Is there a way home?" (canon: no, or nobody has found it; the Junkie says this is home).
- **Site flavour** works by conditions on lines, not by separate trees. Each of these parts has one line per site (`entity.backroomsSite`), and the game shows the one whose condition holds: the greeting, the name, the site's creatures, water, sleep, ways out, the rumour and the answer about home. Shared lines carry the rest.
- **Mechanics** are stated as the handbook and `docs/GUIDE.md` state them, never invented. Examples: a torch, not a candle, for Smilers; a shut door within reach halves night visits; nothing visits a sleeper beside a wanderer; murky water boils clean in a lit fire and salt water never does; Level 8's seeps refill; three Hub doors are always open; one staff door in six in Level 3999 opens onto the Hub.
- **Voices** follow lore.md: survivors dry and weary, the M.E.G. breezy and official, the B.N.T.G. a polite brochure, the Junkies melancholy. The lines nod to the notes ("Read every one... Then pass it on") but never quote them.
