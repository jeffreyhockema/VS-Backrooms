# Level 3999, "The Neon Paradise": research, design and integration plan

Level 3999 is the Backrooms' **way out**: an endless, safe, neon-lit arcade the size of a mall. People come here because the lore says that from here, and almost nowhere else, you can leave. It is reached last. In this mod it is the **capstone of the whole game**: you get there through the deepest levels, earn the way out by completing a List of ten errands across the Backrooms, and walk through a glass door into a car park under a real night sky. It is an ending that a sandbox can survive, because afterwards you wake up at home, in Level 0, the way canon says you do.

Internal id **19** (`WorldLayout.Level3999`), shown as **3999**, tag `3999`, block prefix `l3999-`.

This is a design only; nothing is implemented. Section 9 lists the decisions for the user, each with a recommendation.

## 1. What the lore says

### 1.1 Which Level 3999: there is no Wikidot page, so the canon is the Fandom lineage

- **Wikidot has no Level 3999.** Both `backrooms.wikidot.com/level-3999` and `backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/level-3999` return "This page does not exist yet". Wikidot's numbered levels stop at 999 (its index, `normal-levels-i`, runs 0–999), and it has no Level 399 either.
- **Miraheze has no Level 3999** either (`backrooms.miraheze.org`: missing title).
- The **best-known Level 3999 was born on the Fandom wiki**:
  - "Level 3999: The True Ending", also called "The Neon Paradise". Original author Lazynickname, rewritten by CatRBLX and Pexy.
  - Its move log tells the story: created as "The Promised Land" and moved to Level 3999 (2022-12-18).
  - Deleted and restored several times, then **renumbered to Level 399 on 2023-04-03** ("Level 399: Neon Paradise", rewritten by Goldscar). The old title is now only an "unused redirect" (deleted 2026-06-16).
  - Sources: Fandom API `list=logevents&letitle=Level_3999`; current page `https://backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Level_399`.
  - The pre-2023 text survives on the archive wiki: `https://backrooms-wiki-2021-archival.fandom.com/wiki/Level_3999`. Many fan wikis copy it: the-backrooms-fanmade ("The True Ending"), backrooms-freewriting and bubbynolastname13s ("The Neon Paradise"), backrooms-vertex ("The Neon Arcade").
- **The most widely seen depiction is *Escape the Backrooms*** (Fancy Games, Steam). Its Level 3999 was added in Part 3 and is the game's **true-ending level**. Fandom: `https://escapethebackrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Level_3999`, `/wiki/Endings`, `/wiki/Level_10`, `/wiki/Ticket`.

**Chosen:** the Neon Paradise of the Fandom lineage (the original 3999 text plus its current Level 399 rewrite), with Escape the Backrooms (ETB) for the concrete look and the sequence of the ending.

**Why this one:**
- It is the only Level 3999 with real reach. Every fan wiki and the most-played Backrooms game agree on it: an endless safe arcade, and the exit to reality.
- The other "3999"s are minor fan pages.

### 1.2 The original Fandom Level 3999 (archived)

Source: `https://backrooms-wiki-2021-archival.fandom.com/wiki/Level_3999`.

**Class 0: Safe, Secure, Devoid of Entities.** "The 4000th level, making it the end of Cluster IV." It is famous for one thing: you can exit to reality from here.

- **Look.**
  - An "infinite arcade, with many famous arcade games".
  - "Extremely open, with **windows showing … an outside parking lot**."
  - Machines stand in arcade-themed rooms and at random about the level. Every arcade game ever made is here (Polybius, lost games).
  - Some machines "can make you go to certain levels".
- **Machines.**
  - Most need no money; the coin-op ones are "likely non-functional".
  - **Indestructible, and repaired within the hour** where they stand.
  - Their controls are always different.
- **Habitable.**
  - All food is safe to eat; crates and the like do more good here.
  - People come to stay. The groups are Singularity Point (helps travellers to the exit, sells Almond Water), the Backroom Colonists (cafés, trading) and Oasis Cara (guides, about 9,000 people).
- **The exit (the heart of the page).**
  1. "Walk north till you find a **glass door**." Groups will guide you to one.
  2. Go through it: "you get teleported to **Level 0** and have to complete a random **checklist of ten requests**." These are usually going through a certain level, or drinking so much Almond Water. Each takes anything from minutes to years. You don't age while you do them, but you can die.
  3. Come back and go through the glass doors with the finished list, and you reach the Frontrooms.
  4. "People who have done this report themselves **ascending towards a planet, then blacking out at their house**."
- **Sub-level 3999.1:** "an inverse of this level … extremely dangerous … entities, toxic gases".
- **Entrances:**
  - a purple glitched wall in **Level 11**;
  - a neon blue wall (153), an arcade in a village (1207), a rainbow slide (806), Door 1 of 561, the light from Detention, a subway (1275);
  - luck-based: noclipping into a person, exiting 3998, 3999.1 or 382.
- **Exits:**
  - playing certain games: Spore → 949, DOOM → UAC, Minecraft → 1248 or 3999.391, Terraria → 4382, and so on;
  - the glass door → the Frontrooms, or **Level 0 if you haven't fulfilled the list**;
  - noclipping → 561.

### 1.3 The current Fandom rewrite: Level 399, "Neon Paradise", "The Final Arcade"

Source: `https://backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Level_399`. Its banner and background are still "Level 3999.jpg".

**Class Habitable: Safe, Devoid of Harmful Entities, Sustained Community.**

- **Building.**
  - A building the size of a vast mall, of 49 floors with "colossal" space and **mezzanines**.
  - **Concrete walls, marble-tiled floors**, and the whole level regenerates itself.
  - Colourful arcade machines and computers in neat lines; heavy **neon LED** lighting; modern furniture, retro cabinets.
  - "Lacks non-Euclidean geometry": it can be mapped. The layout is "extremely intuitive", laid out on feng shui principles.
- **Air.**
  - **24 °C, constant.**
  - **Faint background music of mysterious origin, different for each wanderer.**
- **Getting about.** **Elevators and high-speed moving walkways** are common. Areas full of machines are called "game areas".
- **Vending machines** sell Frontrooms snacks: "**One Almond Water bottle must be inserted to receive a snack**." How they refill is a mystery.
- **Prizes.**
  - Playing machines may earn a prize from the **Arcade Attendants**, and harder games give better ones.
  - "The prize range does not have any limits": copies of Object 13 and fragments of Object 100 are rumoured.
- **Lobbies are where wanderers arrive.** They are grand and spacious, with **a reception desk and orderly couches and tables**, and serve as resting lounges (game areas have no seating). Rarely, a train of the Neon Express manifests in one for ten minutes.
- **Food courts.**
  - A **single-file line of stalls** set into the walls: knock-offs of Frontrooms chains, or unique places.
  - Wooden tables and chairs in front.
  - Attendants run them and serve. The food gives mild health and energy, and "may taste slightly funky and artificial".
  - **Some food courts have windows onto the outside world**, possibly the real Frontrooms, possibly innocuous windows. **They cannot be broken.**
- **Mood.**
  - Nostalgia, happiness and contentment; less cortisol. People with severe trauma or serious crimes behind them don't feel it.
  - "Despite these positive sensations, many still report a sense of **loneliness and melancholy**."
- **Sub-zones.**
  - The **Rosatin Hotel**, inside a massive pillar at the level's centre:
    - futuristic and neon, 399 floors, 3,999 rooms;
    - self-cleaning carpet; a key board in each floor's lobby with glowing keychains;
    - rooms that customise themselves;
    - windows onto the pink-cloud "Plane of Clouds".
    - It began as "The Promised Land", the page that became Level 3999 in 2022.
  - The **Neon Natatorium**:
    - follow a sudden smell of the ocean to find it;
    - **iridescent purple pools and hot tubs** with neon LED edges, ceramic tile, never deeper than 3 m;
    - loungers, vending machines, neon slides, showers;
    - about **34 °C**, humid;
    - its location shifts;
    - **Paradis Water** (Almond Water + **Epsom salt** + a helpful bacterium) soothes and heals, and turns back into Almond Water once it leaves the zone.
- **History.**
  - Confirmed in 1993.
  - The "**1994 Neon Blackout**" split off **Level 399.1, "Dilapidated Limbo"**, the level's dark antithesis.
  - The Euphoric Wars: Partygoers attacked it.
  - The Flicker of 2016 brought darkness and entities.
  - The Megacolony (about 24,000 people) runs it.
- **Arcade Attendants (the only endemic entity).**
  - Faceless humanoid robots about 5 ft (152 cm) tall, in **flight-attendant-like** outfits, made of a special plastic.
  - They speak formally through a speaker in the head, and calm you to look at.
  - Docile; they cannot attack. They hand out prizes and serve food.
  - An interview suggests they are dead wanderers' souls.
- **Entrances:**
  - **Level 37: "following or sinking into bright magenta tiles"**;
  - **The End: "Run TRUEEND.EXE in The End's only computer. Locating the computer is hard, good luck."**;
  - a pink crumpled paper of instructions;
  - a train in 154, a vending machine in 699, bowling 20 strikes in 499, getting lost in 595's mall, the Grand Exit of 997.
- **Exits:**
  - **revolving doors** in lobbies, opposite the reception desk → the last level you were at;
  - sand in the Natatorium → 290;
  - **noclipping into a rusted arcade machine → 399.1**;
  - "complete the quests in your **pink crumpled paper** … exit the **grand lobby's revolving door** → [Level 398](https://backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Level_398) (reality)";
  - a chess king in a coffee shop → 397;
  - noclipping → 513.
- **Level 398, "The Cruelest Trick Played on Man"**, is where that reality exit leads. Its text makes the escape a trick: "Make no mistake, the Backrooms wasn't reality". The way out is a promise the Backrooms keeps only in its own way.

### 1.4 Escape the Backrooms: Level 3999

Source: `https://escapethebackrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Level_3999`. **Class 0, no entities.** The note at the elevator reads "THE CODE SHOULD BE SOMEWHERE AROUND HERE…".

- **The way in, from Level 10.** In the fogged wheat of Level 10, five **windmills** each shine a **red light through the fog** toward the next. Switching on the generator inside one turns its light green and lights the next. The level's note: "TURN ON THE WINDMILLS, THE LIGHTS WILL POINT TOWARDS THE WAY OUT". The last light leads to **the parking lot of Level 3999**, an arcade building with a **revolving door**.
- **The layout.**
  - **Entrance** (revolving door, front desk) → a long, damaged, **abandoned arcade hall** → the **mini arcade**, a small room of machines with an **elevator**.
  - Its code, **931**, is printed on the red **Tickets** lying on a table; the ticket is the level's only item.
  - On the ride, a M.E.G. public-information video plays on a TV in the ceiling corner.
  - The elevator goes to Level 0.2. A **STAFF ONLY door** leads to **The Hub**, and the revolving door back to Level 10.
- **Look.**
  - Empty, abandoned, modern: "filled with neon lights and arcade machines lining the walls".
  - "Civilization can be seen just beyond the **impenetrable glass windows**" (playnews.gg guide).
- **The Final Escape ending.**
  - After all four other endings, a locked **ending machine room** beside the mini arcade opens. Its machines display the endings you have earned, and a big M.E.G. machine stands in the middle.
  - Use it, and you turn round to **a human employee telling you they can't keep people in the arcade past ten**. The arcade is a normal arcade now. He unlocks the doors, says it has been a long day, and as the screen goes dark: hopefully yours was better. Credits.
  - You escaped. Fancy's devlog 8 had said leaving would put you in **Buffalo, New York**. The Fandom banner photo of Level 3999 was taken in the **Regal Elmwood Center, Buffalo**.
- **The Part 3 placeholder ending:** walk into the dark of the arcade hall, and the lights come on, one after another, down an endless hall.
- **Achievements:** "Escape Level 3999", "Escaped!".

## 3. Design

### 3.1 The experience

- **Arrival.** You come in through a revolving door into a carpeted lobby. The carpet is black with neon squiggles. Couches stand in neat rows; a reception desk glows lilac. Music is playing, soft and synthetic, and nobody else seems to hear quite the same tune.
- **The concourse.** Beyond the lobby a concourse runs off further than you can see under arches of pink and cyan neon. Moving walkways hum down its middle. Mezzanine balconies overhang it, lined with more cabinets.
- **Attendants.** A faceless attendant in a plum uniform bows.
- **Plenty.** Nothing here wants to hurt you. There is food, if you play for it. For the first time in a long time you could stop.
- **The pull.** Then you notice the windows: a car park, under street lamps, under a sky. You cannot break them. The attendants all point the same way, north, to the glass doors.

Design goals, in order:
1. **The finale.** The deepest, hardest to reach, most beautiful level, and the only one that ends something.
2. **It must read instantly as the Neon Paradise**: black-light carpet, neon tubes, rows of cabinets, a food court.
3. **Safe but not empty.** It is a place to stop, to earn, to trade and to plan the List.
4. **"Senseless but strict" turned to "intuitive"**: canon says this level can be mapped, so it is a strict, legible mall plan.

### 3.2 Structure (a skeleton above the cell, like Level 37)

The level is planned in **blocks of 4×4 cells (128 blocks)**, each cached with `PlanCache` from its id (salt 0x1902).

**Concourses (the spine)** run along every block line, west–east and north–south, forever.
- 12 wide, from −6 to +5 about the line.
- A **two-storey atrium** 18 high: a mezzanine gallery 3 wide on each side (floor slab at rel 7) above the shopfronts, with glass balustrades.
- Down the middle, two **moving walkways** 2 wide, running in opposite directions. Each runs a full 128-block stretch between junctions and ends in combplates 4 blocks short of each junction.
- Between them, benches and planters, and a **neon arch** every 16 blocks: wall tubes up both mezzanine faces and a ceiling tube across.
- A **bridge** across the concourse at mezzanine level every 64 blocks, at the half-block point.

**Junctions** (where concourses cross) are a 24×24 atrium plaza:
- a giant hanging **"3999" neon sign** at the centre;
- **escalators** up to the mezzanine on two opposite sides (7 steps, rel 1 to 7);
- four lit **directory boards**, which mark the way out (section 4.1).

**Inside each block**, 116×116 between the concourses:
- A cross of **service corridors** splits it into four **quadrants** of 56×56. The corridors are back-of-house: 3 wide, grey concrete, fluorescent tubes, storerooms.
- Each quadrant is a **unit** that fronts onto the two concourses beside it, with a glass shopfront and a 6-wide entrance under a neon sign. Its back walls hold steel STAFF ONLY doors onto the service corridors.
- Two neighbouring quadrants may merge into one big unit (natatorium, grand game hall).

**"Strict, legible, mappable"** (canon) in numbers:
- every unit is symmetric about one axis;
- cabinet rows run parallel to the unit's long side;
- every entrance is centred on its frontage;
- every quadrant's neon colour is one of the six, picked by hash.
- Navigation therefore works as in a real mall: concourse, junction, directory.

**Mezzanines.** A tile is one of three vertical profiles, kept per tile (field use per CONTRACT; suggested: `Tile.Height` = ceiling height, `Tile.Stack` = 1 for a mezzanine floor at rel 7):

| Profile | Ground storey | Mezzanine | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hall** | air rel 1–6 | none | rel 7, solid above |
| **Atrium** | open rel 1–17 | none | rel 18 |
| **Gallery** | air rel 1–6 | slab at 7, air 8–17 | rel 18 |

- Walls of atrium-height units run the full 18.
- **Escalators** are `Tile.Stair` 1–7 climbing toward `Pier`, under an opening in the slab.
- **Balustrades** stand on every gallery tile that borders an atrium tile. They are drawn on the upper floor by the generator, which looks at the neighbours.
- **Arrivals are always on the ground floor**, so the 2D harness checks stay valid. The mezzanine is reachable only by escalator; the harness `arcade` mode checks each gallery ring has one.

### 3.3 The Frontrooms car park (the centre)

At the region's centre, in place of the block `(0, 0)`:
- **A car park 116×116, open to the real sky**: the only open sky in 3999.
- Its south side is **the arcade's façade**: stucco and brick, a big neon sign **"PARADISE"**, the glass doors (from outside) and one **revolving door**.
- The other three sides are a **painted backdrop** 59 blocks high (to the top of the world), in the manner of Level 94's: a town at night (house rows, trees, a lit petrol-station sign, a highway overpass, a green highway sign "BUFFALO 12"), and above it a night sky fading to dawn at the bottom.
- Inside:
  - asphalt with white bay lines, kerbs, grass medians of real soil;
  - sodium street lamps (orange, light 15);
  - a dozen parked cars (the city's car props);
  - a **bus shelter** with a timetable, and a **payphone**.
- **A road** leaves through a gap in the north backdrop into a bank of fog: **the horizon** (section 4.3).
- It is reachable **only by escaping** (section 4). The windows of the four blocks round it are real windows onto it, which makes canon's "windows showing an outside parking lot" literally true near the centre.

### 3.4 Light, fog and sound

## 4. The way out: the List, the glass doors and the Ending

This is the one new system, and the reason the level exists. It gives a Vintage Story sandbox an **ending it can survive**: a goal at the top of the game that sends you back through every level you know, a moment when you reach it, and a world that goes on afterwards.

### 4.1 Finding the glass doors

- **Glass doors** stand only in **Grand Lobbies**, always in their north wall. Canon: "walk north till you find a glass door".
- **Ways to find one:**
  - **Directory boards** (junctions, lobbies): right-click marks the nearest Grand Lobby, the nearest food court and the nearest lobby on your map, and says in chat which way and how far ("The way out: north-north-east, about 420 paces").
  - **Attendants** point and mark (section 3.9). Canon: groups guide you.
  - Wanderers' notes found in 3999 tell of a Grand Lobby.

### 4.2 The List

1. **The first time anyone goes through a glass door** without a List, the door *takes* them (canon):
   - a white flash, then they wake in **Level 0** (one-way, fixed per door: rule 1);
   - in their hand is **the List**, a crumpled pink paper (canon).
   - Message: "You are not finished. The paper in your hand says what is left."
   - (Level 0 is the nearest place to the world you came from: the survival-loop plan's own lore note.)
2. **The List** is ten errands, written for you alone, from `hash(seed, playerUID, escapes)`. They are kept on the player (`WatchedAttributes` tree `backrooms:list`); the paper only shows them.
   - Hover: the tasks with ticks.
   - Right-click: the tasks in chat.
   - Lose it, and any reception desk in 3999 gives you a copy.
   - Every task counts only after the List is given.

| # | Kind | Pool (draws without repeats) | Checked by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | **Set foot in** an easy level | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | the level-arrival hook (the place `AtmosphereSystem.AnnounceLevel` already fires; server side: `ThresholdSystem`'s per-player `lastSpot` level change) |
| 2 | **Set foot in** a deep level | 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 | same |
| 1 | **Set foot in** a far place | 6.1, 11, 37, 94; plus Fun, the End, the Hub, 974 and ! once they exist | same |
| 1 | **Find** a place | the Manila Room, the Beverly Room, the 9th Road, the Snackrooms, the Sanguine Reservoir, the King's castle | zone predicates the layout already has |
| 2 | **Bring** a thing | Epsom salt (37), Almond Milk (94), Firesalt, a seed packet, surveyor's chalk, a pry bar, a hotel bedsheet (linen), a jar of honey (Level 10 skeps) | carried through the glass door, then taken ("An attendant takes them from you, and thanks you.") |
| 1 | **Drink** Almond Water | 8–15 bottles | the `BackroomsAlmondWater` eat hook counts |

Canon says ten requests, from minutes to years. This list is days of play: a tour of the whole game, including at least one deep place.

3. **Going back through a glass door with an unfinished List**: the door stays shut ("Not yet. 7 of 10 done."). It never sends you back to Level 0 twice.

### 4.3 The Ending ("closing time")

**Going through a glass door with a finished List:**

1. **Closing time** (about 12 s, in place, the player free to look about):
   - the music winds down;
   - a deep **power-down** clunk: the neon round you dims (client-side, AtmosphereSystem: fog fades to a flat neutral grey-white, scene brightness up);
   - **ordinary fluorescent tubes plink on**;
   - the arcade is just an arcade.
   - An attendant's chime, then a human voice in chat: three lines in this mod's own words, after ETB's ending (lang `l3999-ending-1..3`, section 6.6).
2. The glass doors open with a click. **A white fade**, and you stand **in the car park**, facing out, under the sky (vanilla time; at night the stars).
   - Title: **"You escaped the Backrooms"**, subtitle "Day {n}" (the arrival-banner mechanism, `AnnounceLevel` with a custom key).
   - **Server broadcast:** "{player} found the way out, on day {n}."
   - The List becomes **a neon wristband** (`l3999-wristband`, "Escaped on day {n}", a keepsake).
   - The player is marked `backrooms:escaped` (with day and count).
3. **The Frontrooms car park** is yours as long as you like:
   - the safest place in the mod: stability 1.5, nothing hostile, a real sky, rain;
   - you can sit at the bus stop; the payphone gives a dial tone and nobody answers.
   - The **revolving door** in the façade goes back into the arcade, to the Grand Lobby nearest the centre (two-way). From then on that Grand Lobby's glass doors take you, an escapee, straight back out to the car park.
4. **The horizon.** Walk down the road into the fog:
   - you are **lifted**: the player rises slowly into the sky for about 6 s (server: no gravity, upward motion; the stars brighten);
   - the screen whitens, and **you wake at home**: your anchor in Level 0, where the anchor rule always puts you.
   - Title: "You wake up at home."
   - This is canon twice over: "ascending towards a planet, then blacking out at their house" (original), and the reality exit as "the cruelest trick" (current). You did get out. You woke up where you always do.
5. **Afterwards**:
   - the wristband stays;
   - the handbook's Ways Between Levels lists the car park;
   - a **new List** can be had from any glass door, for another escape (each counted: "Escapes: 2").
   - **Recommended reward (decision 3):** a temporal gear also takes hold **in the Frontrooms car park** for an escapee, so they can make a home under the real sky, on real soil, with real sunlight for crops. The anchor rule is untouched for everyone who has not escaped.

Nothing here ends the game, takes anything away or changes the world for other players.

## 8. Risks

- **Moving walkways on clients.** Player movement is client-side, so the push must run in the client's physics too. **Fallback:** `walkspeedmultiplier` 1.8 on the tread (no direction, but fast).
- **Oversized shapes** (revolving door, signs, sails) may be culled at chunk edges. Keep them within ±1 block of their own, and test early in a client.
- **The ending's client effects**: fades, titles, the ascent. If the ascent's motion fights client physics, teleport upward in short steps under a white overlay instead.
- **The List's long reach**: a task naming a level the world can't reach (a new level unbuilt) would block the escape. `listgen` must check against the live registry, and a List never names a level that is not implemented.
- **Neon light cost**: thousands of coloured light sources. `WorldLighting` caches per cell. Measure chunk time with the `timing` mode; aim for under 40 ms per chunk.
- **Byte-identity of Levels 10, 11 and 37.** The new features must be added "over the top" with their own Rng, as the city's cinemas were.
