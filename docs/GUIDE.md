# Backrooms: Survival Story — Player Guide

Everything the mod adds, level by level. It is full of spoilers: if you would rather find things out for yourself, the in-game handbook (H) and the notes you find will tell you enough to survive.

## Playing

Create a new world and pick the **Backrooms: Survival Story** playstyle. It only affects worlds created with that playstyle. Normal worlds are untouched.

- **Map (M / F6):** the map only shows what you have actually seen. It reveals the area around you along your line of sight, so walls block it. Explored areas are remembered between sessions, so you can find your way back.
- **Handbook (H):** in a Backrooms world the Guides tab is rewritten for the Backrooms, in the voice of the vanilla guides. The Starter Guide, the Progression Guide and Reality Stability replace their vanilla pages; eight pages are added after the Progression Guide (Home and Expeditions; Finding the Way; Salvage; Food, Heat and Cold; The Levels: 0 to 11; The Levels: Beyond the Rings; Ways Between Levels; What Lives Here); and every other vanilla guide (knapping, farming, smelting, steelmaking…) keeps its text but opens with an "In the Backrooms" preface saying what is different here. Other worlds keep the vanilla handbook.
- **Tutorial:** the Tutorials tab holds *Backrooms: First Steps*, a 15-step quick start on the game's own tutorial system (read the pocket note, open the boxes, glass, cutting, shiv, bags, scraper, tinder, torch, map, handbook) that replaces the vanilla First Steps in Backrooms worlds and starts by itself the first time you join one (`.tutorial hud` hides it; your progress is kept on your own computer).
- **Starting waypoint:** "Where I woke up" marks your starting point.
- **Your own waypoints:** right-click the map to add one.
- **Waypoints made for you:** every gate you walk through, and every elevator, stairwell, door or pit you take (with the way back, where there is one), is marked on your map the first time. Reading a wanderer's note marks the place it tells of.
- **Less map, or none:** the *Backrooms map* world setting can take the waypoints made for you away, or the whole map (see the world settings below).
- **Notes and the journal (J):** wanderers' notes say what earlier wanderers learned, in order, and every note read is kept in your journal (see *Notes and the journal* below).
- **Dark areas** are easier to explore with a candle.
- **Arriving in a level** shows its name, with a word of advice for the dangerous ones.
- **Surveyor's chalk** (sometimes found in crumbled ceiling tiles, or crafted from a vanilla chalk stone) marks walls with an arrow, a cross or a circle. Choose the mark with the tool mode key (F), and click a mark again to change it. Chalk marks show bright white on the map.

## Surviving

### Home and expeditions

The Backrooms are survived from a home, in expeditions.

- **You only ever wake up in Level 0.** Die anywhere and you wake in the Lobby, and what you carried stays where you fell. A temporal gear still moves where you wake, but only within Level 0; elsewhere it will not take hold and is not used up. Everything a home needs (a door, a chest, fire, seeds, soil) has to be brought back from deeper levels.
- **The walls between levels are solid, but for gates.** A gate (a doorway through) comes every few hundred blocks along a boundary. Chalk arrows on the walls nearby point the way to one, and wanderers' notes tell of them. Walking the wall looking for one is walking the most dangerous ground of a level.
- **Food is scarce.** Most supply boxes and crates were gone through long ago: a torn box or an emptied crate can be told from across the room and never holds food. Level 0's boxes were left in twos and threes in the corners of rooms, about one cell in six (one in three or four in the blackouts), plus the caches at the hearts of dens; a sealed box holds some five minutes of walking's food. The deeper the level, the less is picked over; rooms behind locked doors and the hearts of dens were never touched. Hunger runs at 125%.
- **Heat and cold.** From 30 °C up you go hungry faster, up to half again as fast (Levels 2 and 3, Level 5's boiler halls). Levels 6 and 8 are cold enough to want clothes. Level 0's blackout zones are cold and damp, so food keeps there.
- **Farming at home.** Crops grow in Level 0 under the ceiling lights (light 15 under a working troffer gives about 60% of daylight growth) on soil from potted plants, watered from a flooded room or a bucket. Seed packets come from Level 9's sheds and kitchens and, rarely, supply boxes.

### Notes and the journal

- **A note is blank until it is read.** What it says is the next entry due in that player's own sequence: the first note anyone reads is always the same, and **the series comes in order**. After the first three, notes alternate between *The Wanderer's Way* (25 entries carrying a player from waking to the far levels, some waiting until you have been somewhere) and the track of the level the note is read in (one per level, 3 to 9 entries, Levels 0 to 188).
- **A cast of writers** carries them: Dez the scavenger, A. Lindqvist the surveyor, M.E.G. Field Team 9 (their count falls from six to one), Tom writing to his wife Ruth, Nell the grower, and many one-off hands.
- **Folded notes** (a rarer paper) give the next entry of the level's rare track, then of a general one: exact routes, odds and rules. **Scraps** of flavour come when the tracks run dry; after everything, a note is just its map mark.
- **Map marks:** every note marks by its paper (door, way, cache, refuge); some entries also mark a place of their own (the Manila Room, a den, a manhole, the barn wall, an EXIT door that opens, a Hub entrance, a Red Room).
- **A note is kept when read** and remembers its readers (an attribute on the item). To whoever has read it, it is named "(read)" and reading it again only says it is in their journal; anyone else reading it gets the next entry in their own sequence, and marks of their own. One paper, one read per player, so notes can be shared on a server (a directions note that found nothing forgets its reader, to be read elsewhere). Fresh notes stack as before; read ones stack with notes read by the same players.
- **The journal** (J, or `.journal`) keeps every note read (of Directions, the last 40), per player on the server (it survives death and relog): The Wanderer's Way, the levels in order, Folded notes, Scraps, Directions; each with where and when it was read, and *Show on map* / *Mark again*.

### You are a scavenger

What you take is mostly salvage. The building itself can be broken (to shape a home, or to cut a way through), but it breaks *down*, never into blocks you can set up again; and where it holds the levels apart it does not break at all.

- **The building breaks with the tool its material calls for, into scraps:**
  - Drywall, plaster and wallpaper (Levels 0, 4, 5, the houses): any tool, an axe best; bare hands at a seventh of the pace. Mostly dust; now and then a stick or a wallpaper strip.
  - Boards, panelling, floorboards and roofs: the same. Sticks, a little firewood, rarely a plank.
  - Concrete, brick, tile, marble and every floor slab: a copper pickaxe, like rock. **Rubble**; carpeted floors a few carpet fibres. Four rubble make a block of **rubble masonry** to build with.
  - Pipes, railings, poles and machinery: a copper pickaxe. Metal brackets, a little scrap wire from anything wired, now and then a copper or lead bit from pipes.
  - Glass: anything. Glass shards.
  - Vanilla rock and ore (Level 8, the cliffs) and soil break as in any world.
- Furniture and clutter are not the building: a minibar, a cake tin or a bin breaks like any block and gives what is in it, and what you made or won (rubble masonry, a plush or neon lamp from a prize counter) comes back whole.
- The walls between levels break like any other wall of their material: the gates are the easy way through, not the only one.
- **Never broken** (the block info says so):
  - Each level's roof: from its ordinary ceiling height up to the mantle cap, the blocks with nothing open above them. Level 0's ceiling tiles and crawlspace can come down; the slab over the crawlspace cannot. On a level with one plain ceiling, that ceiling is the roof. None under the open sky.
  - The wall between two levels from the lower of their roof heights up (below that it breaks like any wall of its kind, so it can be broken through or tunnelled under).
  - Doors, elevators, vents, thin spots, vending machines, fountains and other dispensers, and landmarks.
  - Two buildings whole: Kitty's House in Level 974 (only its furniture breaks), and the hotel of Level 188, its walls, floors, windows, doors, stairs, deck and courtyard (only what is in its rooms breaks).
- Lights, lit or dead, break for glass shards, a bracket and some wire. Wrecked cars break with a copper pickaxe, the whole car at once, for brackets, wire, glass and a little copper.
- **The ground is vanilla's:** under every floor are 8 blocks of concrete, then the ground Vintage Story lays down in any world, from its own generators: the shape of the land (with its sea level put at the floor), rock strata, caves, and ore, coal, minerals and gems. Where the land lay lower than the foundations it is filled up with its own rock; where it rose higher the levels replace it. Nothing of the surface is borrowed (no soil, plants, trees, ruins or story structures), nor the soil deposits (clay, peat, gravel), which would break out in the open levels' lawns and fields.

- **Loose objects break as normal:** supply boxes, crates, furniture, ceiling tiles and dead light fixtures.
  - Dead fixtures give glass shards, and sometimes a bracket or wire.
  - Ceiling tiles mostly give nothing, but the void above sometimes hides scrap wire, a metal bracket or rotten wood.
- **Walls and carpet can be cut:** right-click papered walls or carpet with a blade to cut away wallpaper strips or carpet fibres. The wall or floor stays, visibly bare.
  - A glass shard works as a blade, but may snap each time.
  - The glass shiv and the improvised scraper are made for it: they peel the paper and lift the pile cleanly, two strips or fibres a cut, where any other knife (a flint one too) gives one. Keep one after you have flint.

### A Vintage Story player wakes in the Backrooms

Breaking things down feeds Vintage Story's own progression, so the usual path from knapped flint to bronze still works. What changes is where each material comes from.

- **Level 0 (the Lobby):** wanderers' supply boxes hold sticks and dry grass, but flint only about one time in twenty: the Lobby's blades are the glass shiv and the scraper, and fire starts with tinder cut from the wallpaper. Make a fire starter, torches and a firepit. Furniture breaks into planks, sticks and nails, so a chest or a barrel is possible before you own a saw.
- **Level 1:** where flint is found: the crumbling concrete leaves loose stones and flint about, and crates and shelves hold sticks, tinder, flint, charcoal and brackets. Knap a flint knife and axe. Toolboxes (here, in Level 2's tunnels and Level 9's garages) sometimes hold a copper hammer, chisel, saw or knife.
- **Levels 2 and 3:** cable drums, pipe stacks, fans and machine cabinets give copper and lead bits. Scrap wire smelts to copper. The flooded tunnel under Level 3 is floored with clay for pottery, moulds and a crucible. The generator rooms have coal heaped beside them, enough to smelt with.
- **Level 4:** office furniture gives lumber and paper. Fridges hold fruit and cheese. Storerooms sometimes hide a supply box. The dead potted plants give soil and pottery shards: pounded to grit with a stone and worked into soil, about four plants make one blue clay, a slow way to clay for whoever is not ready for Level 3's tunnel.
- **Level 5:** coal piles in the boiler halls. The beds come away as real beds you can sleep in. Wardrobes and suitcases hold clothes. Dining tables, curio cabinets and service trolleys give silver; luggage carts, ashtray stands and chandeliers give brass (candelabras stand in Level 3's Sanctums and the city).
- **Level 7:** driftwood is firewood, and the sea has fish.
- **Level 8:** real ore veins (copper, tin, zinc, bismuth, iron, gold, lead, sulfur, coal…; no silver) run through the strata, with loose ores and stones on the cave floors and clay in places. Limestone and chalk grind to lime, and halite grinds to salt.
- **Level 9:** houses to strip, wardrobes and dressers of clothes, berry bushes gone wild in the gardens, and wildlife: hares, foxes and raccoons.
- **Level 10:** crops ready to harvest, livestock in the paddocks, cattails at the ponds and clay in their beds, bee skeps for honey and wax, and an old quern in the barns.
- **Level 37:** Epsom salt for poultices; somewhere to heal.
- **Level 94:** the ground can be dug: its turf gives poor soil, the modelling clay under it gives red clay (with a layer of blue clay a little way down), enough for all the pottery you want, and three to seven blocks under the turf run seams of **fire clay** (five deep, over about half the hills): the Backrooms' only fire clay, and so the only road to fire bricks, the bloomery, iron and steel. Iceboxes, Almond Milk and fountains of Almond Water feed you while you are there.
- **Level !:** a test of food, armour weight and nerve rather than a place to gather: sprinting is the only answer (armour slows you, and the horde with you). Energy Bars give a second wind; caught, you lose everything you carried.
- **Level Fun:** the richest larder, guarded: cakes, cookies, pumpkins, gifts of wanderers' gear, linen from tablecloths. Poultices and bandages answer the Partygoers' bite; fire keeps them off.
- **The End:** no food, but the furniture of a home (vanilla chairs, tables, ladders, shelves), paper and books, some of them written in.
- **The Hub:** cold (6 °C in the Crossroads, 2 °C out where its lamps die, milder in the Tower; so clothes) and foodless, but safe, and Level Keys turn it into a way back to levels you have earned.
- **Level 974:** a real bed, water from the taps, a fridge and pantry that refill, and Kitty's dresser of clothes.
- **Level 3999:** a daily income of tickets (once per machine per day, 40 a day) for prizes up to a temporal gear; tray meals; vending machines that turn Almond Water into food; salvage that feeds the crucible (circuit boards to copper, coin mechanisms to brass) and the furnace (plastic), and neon lamps to craft.
- **Level 188:** clothes, cloth and linen from the rooms; a little breakfast; mostly a junction, read from its windows.

Some limits:

- The Level 10 crops grow in old, exhausted farmland, so seeds planted back in it will not grow. Hoe fresh soil instead.
- Temporal gears are very rare: a supply box gives one about once in a hundred.

### Reality Stability

The gear on the HUD shows how firmly you are anchored. It is Vintage Story's temporal stability, with the Backrooms' own geography.

- **Each area has its own stability.** Where it is low the gear turns backwards and your stability drains. When yours is low the world distorts, and at zero you take damage. Below about a third the air of whatever level you are in thickens, goes a dull brown-grey and dims, more the lower you fall, and clears as you recover.
  - Kind places: the Manila Room, the Level 1 car park, all of Level 4, the Beverly Room, Level !'s Safe Zone, The End's central hall, the Hub, Kitty's House for its first 18 hours, Level 3999 wherever its neon is lit (the lobbies most), and Level 188's guest rooms.
  - Bad places: Level 0's blackout zones and above all its Red Rooms, Level 2, Level 3 (worst in its flooded tunnels), the hotel corridors and above all the boiler halls, Level ! outside the Safe Zone, all of Level Fun (and its Party Fever lowers your cap), The End away from its middle and after two hours there, Kitty's House after 18 hours, Level 3999's failing neon and its Neon Blackout (3999.1), Level 188's corridors, courtyard and roof (most of all near a beckoning window).
- **Almond Water** restores some stability when you drink it.
- **Reality Shifts:** about once every 30 in-game hours the lights fail for a few hours. Everything beyond a few steps is lost in black, and stability drains everywhere.

### Things that live here

The Backrooms are not empty, but what lives in them is there to be heard and glimpsed far more than fought. The handbook page *What Lives Here* covers them too.

- **Danger rises towards the edge of each level.** In the heart of a level you hear things down the hall and see things far off that leave when you come near. Further out, things follow at a distance. Near the edge into the next level, and near its elevators, stairs, pits and noclip walls, they hunt.
- **They come bolder** when you stand in the dark, when your Reality Stability is low, when you run, and during a Reality Shift. After a close call you are left alone for a while: a minute and a half in Level 0, less the deeper you go.
- **Exposure:** the longer you are out without resting, the bolder they come (up to +0.3 menace after ten in-game hours). Resting is a minute by a lit firepit or burning Firesalt, beside a wanderer, in a safe place, asleep in a bed, or at home (within 48 blocks of where you wake, in Level 0).
- **Noise:** breaking something open, forcing a door and running all carry. Noise adds to the menace and dies away within twenty seconds.
- **Dens:** about one cell in 25 in the heart of Levels 1–3, 5, 8 and 9, and one in 12 further out (one in four of Level 0's blackout cells), is a den: a Hound pack over a pile of bones, a Deathmoth hive, a rat warren, a Skin-Stealer's lair, or a Smiler's dark. Within 18 blocks of its heart a den is as dangerous as a level's edge, and what lives there is what comes. Chalk crosses on the walls round it warn you off; two or three sealed boxes sit at its heart. In Level 2's tunnels, where a whole stretch may be dead, a warren has candle stubs burning under its crosses and by its cache; a Smiler's dark stays dark.
- **At the edge**, hunters are put down between you and the gate you are heading for, and anything stalking you gives chase once you run.
- **Level 0**'s heart stays empty but in a Reality Shift, when Smilers watch it from the dark. Further out, things watch and follow (the Bacteria, Smilers in the dark, Hounds at the edge), but nothing hunts there except in the Red Rooms.
- **Safe places:** the Manila Room, Level 3's Sanctums, the Beverly Room, the Snackrooms, the 9th Road and its camps, Level 37 (but for the dark of its Reservoir), Level 94 by day or indoors (outside its castle), the Hub, Kitty's House, The End (but for Smiler glimpses in its collapses), Level 3999 outside its Blackout, a Level 188 guest room with its door shut (unless its window is one of the Windows that day). Level 4's heart and Level 10's fields are calm. In Level 11's streets nothing attacks unless provoked; the subway below is not so kind.

- **Flickerings:** out towards the edge of Level 1 the lights of a section sometimes fail for a minute or two, and a pack of Hounds hunts you until they return.
- **Wanderers** who trade keep to camps in Level 1, outposts in Level 4, the 9th Road, the city, The End's Home rooms, the Hub's outposts and Level 3999's lobbies, about one every kilometre or two of travel: the vanilla trader (right-click to talk, then ask to trade), who never fights back, with a Backrooms trade list (salvage and hides bought; Almond Water, Firesalt, candles, bandages, seeds sold, for rusty gears). Nothing hostile closes in on someone beside one.
- **Firesalt** is found in the coal heaped by Level 3's generators and Level 5's boilers, and from Level 8 can be made (salt + powdered sulfur = 2). Set down, it burns for half a day and nothing hostile comes within about 8 blocks.
- **Apart from the wanderers who trade, entities are never kept with the world.** They are put down out of sight for one player and despawn once they leave.
- **World settings** under Survival challenges when creating a world: *Backrooms entities* (Off: sounds only, Low, Normal, High), *Backrooms scarcity* (Plentiful: twice the food, Normal, Sparse: 0.6 times the food, Scarce: about a third). It scales everything you can eat or drink in every box, crate, cupboard and fridge, how much vending machines, water coolers, fountains and Kitty's fridge hold and how soon they refill, and Level 10's livestock. In new worlds on Sparse or Scarce, Level 9's gardens and Level 10's crops and berry bushes are thinner too (set by the scarcity the world was created with; changing it later doesn't change them). Seeds, notes and tools are not affected. And *Backrooms map*:
  - **Seen areas** (the default): the map as described above.
  - **Seen areas, no waypoints:** the map still fills in as you explore, but nothing is marked for you, not even where you woke up or where you died (vanilla's death waypoint is off too). Add your own waypoints. Notes still give a direction and a number of paces, and your journal keeps them; registers, directories and the like tell you the direction and paces in chat instead of marking the map.
  - **None:** no world map and no minimap; the map keys do nothing and waypoints can't be made. Notes, registers and directories tell you the way as above. Chalk is how you find your way back, and crumbled ceiling tiles and lost backpacks give half as much again of it. A player granted vanilla's `allowMap` privilege still sees the map, but the Backrooms treat the world as mapless for them too: nothing is marked, and the tutorial is the no-map one.
  - In both, the First Steps tutorial's note and map steps are worded for the setting, and The End's computers tell you the way out instead of marking it.
- **Level size**, under World generation when creating a world: *Backrooms level size* (50%, 75%, 100%, the default, 150% or 200%) sets how wide the rings of levels are, so how far you go through each level before the next begins. Only the distance changes: the rooms, halls and buildings of every level are as big as ever, Level 10 still goes on forever, and the city and the other far places lie where they always do. At 50% and 75% no level is ever thinner anywhere than the thinnest stretch of a 100% world; at 150% and 200% in a map smaller than the default million blocks across, the rings are held back a kilometre and a half from the city where they would otherwise run into it, so in those directions they are not as much wider (but never narrower than at 100%). It is fixed when the world is created: changing it later changes nothing, and worlds made before 0.514.0 are 100%.

### The topology lies

The rings are only how the world is built; the network of ways between the levels is the real map. It follows two rules, so you can learn it, mark it on your map and build routes out of what you have found.

**Every threshold always leads to the same place.** Where it leads is fixed the first time anyone goes through it, and saved with the world. A threshold is one thing however big it is: both halves of a gate, every step of a stairwell and every part of a lake's bottom lead to the same spot.

**If you walked through it, you can walk back. If you fell, dived or were taken, you cannot.**

- **Two-way thresholds with a partner:** an elevator arrives beside another elevator, a stairwell on another stairwell, a door beside a locked door. That partner becomes the way back, whatever it would have led to otherwise. Go through it and you come out where you left. (The stairwells of Levels 1 and 4 pair like the floors of a real building: go up a flight and you come out on the landing at the top of another stairwell's flight down, as if you had just come up it; go down and you come out at the foot of another stairwell's flight up, as if you had just come down it; either way you face away from the stairs, and turning round takes you back. Level 1's stairwells are each on a floor, painted on the landing's wall, from P5 at the top down to P1 and on to B1, B2 and B3: up comes out a floor higher, down a floor lower. Climb Level 1's stairs to nowhere and you come out on the landing at the top of a stairwell's flight down, or now and then standing near the top of a Level 4 stairwell's flight, facing down it; that one takes you nowhere until you have stepped off it, so walk down and come back up to go back.)
- **Doors and elevators without a partner** leave one of their kind instead (from 0.514.0, the first time anyone goes through): you step out of a door or an elevator in a wall nearby, in the level's own style (a wooden door or a lift in Level 0, a steel door or a freight lift in Levels 1 and 3, a steel door in Level 2, an office door or a lift in Level 4, a guest-room door or a lift in Level 5, a POOL door in Level 37), and using it takes you back. Where there is no plain stretch of wall near enough, or the level has no doors of its own, it leaves a thin spot, as below.
- **Other two-way thresholds without a partner** leave a **thin spot** where they put you down: a pale, wavering seam in the air that hums like a translocator. Right-click it to step back through. It stays there for good.
- **One-way thresholds** drop you somewhere fixed, with no way back from there.
- **The screen goes dark** the moment you go through any threshold and clears when you are put down. Where you are going may have to be made first, which can take a few seconds, so the dark is the Backrooms working, not the game hanging.

This is how Vintage Story's own translocators behave: a repaired one picks its exit once, a partner appears at the far end, and the pair is fixed from then on.

## How the levels fit together

![Map of the rings around where you wake](levels-overview.png)

*The rings of one world (seed 12345), about 23 km across, one pixel per 32 blocks. The far places lie further out still and are not shown.*

The levels nest like blobs within blobs around the spawn point. Level 0 runs somewhere between about 450 and 3,000 blocks from spawn to its edge, depending on the direction you walk. Each later ring is roughly 450 to 800 blocks across on average, so Level 10 lies something like 4 to 9 km out, and then goes on forever. (Those are the distances at the usual *Backrooms level size*; at 50% or 200% they are about half or twice as far.)

Where two levels meet the wall is solid but for **gates**: within each 320-block square of the world, each stretch of boundary between the same two levels has exactly one, so an island of one level inside another always has a way in. If a gate's doorway would open onto the back wall of a room or a locked room, a short way is broken through to open floor. Level 8 still opens only where the 9th Road runs through, the open-sky levels have no wall between them, and the places you can only noclip into (the city, Level 37, Level 94) have no way through their rims at all.

No ring is a circle. Each one is stretched and lopsided in its own way: a level may end after a few hundred blocks in one direction and go on for several times that in another, and the short way through one level need not line up with the short way through the next. Finding the way on is a matter of mapping, supplies, and luck.

Level 6.1 is not a ring: it is rare sealed cells scattered through Level 6. Where two levels meet there is a full-height wall; its gates are lined up with the corridors on either side where there are any.

Each level looks like itself right up to the wall it shares with the next: the wall is two thick, each side with its own face, and the way through a gate is a short passage with the next level's doorway at its far end. Only that passage carries a touch of the next level: Level 0's carpet is worn to the concrete in the doorway to Level 1, and Level 4's carpet is the hotel's red in the doorway to Level 5.

In worlds made before 0.514.0, the wall is one thick, so one side of it shows the next level's face, and some areas next to a boundary blend, as the wikis describe the transitions:

| Boundary | What blends |

| Level 0 to 1 | Level 0's carpet wears through to concrete and ceiling tiles go missing. |
| Level 1 to 2 | Pipes start running along Level 1's ceiling. |
| Level 2 to 3 | Level 3 keeps Level 2's pipe bundles instead of cables. |
| Level 4 to 5 | The office carpet turns to hotel red and wood panelling creeps in: all of it within about five blocks of the hotel, fraying out to grey office about fourteen blocks from it. |

## Level 0: the Lobby

The rules come from the original 2002 photos behind the 2019 creepypasta, the Backrooms Fandom and Wikidot wikis, and Kane Pixels' depiction.

- **Walls and floor:**
  - one wallpaper everywhere, as in Kane Parsons' footage and the film: dingy pale yellow with the photo's beaded-line and chevron stripes, over a thin dark baseboard;
  - brownish-beige Berber carpet with water stains, and a fissured drop ceiling 3 blocks up.
- **Lights:** recessed fluorescent troffers in a regular grid. Their spacing and orientation change between areas.
  - Most rooms are bright and even.
  - Some are dim, some have failing lights that stutter, buzz or die and flash back on (with a crackle), and a few have gone dark.
  - The hum fades where the lights are dead.
- **Layout:** a labyrinth of rooms and hallways, after Kane Pixels' footage and the 2026 film ("one long corridor that turns into a never-ending labyrinth").
  - The level is laid out in districts of about 44 blocks, staggered like brickwork so no wall lines up with the next. Walls between districts are gapped every dozen blocks or so.
  - In each district, rooms are split off by straight walls (some stop short or have a wide break, so rooms run into each other round corners). Then two- or three-wide hallways wind in from the gaps: they run 6–16 blocks (now and then up to ~26), turn hard, zigzag on, and open into a big room, join another hallway or end blind (some with a chalked cross).
  - Every room and hallway is joined to the rest by doorless openings, many more than needed, so rooms open into rooms. No straight hallway runs much past 25 blocks; only pillar halls give long views.
  - Set pieces among the rooms: a big empty room with free-standing partitions like the photo, rows of identical small rooms off a corridor, a room of pits. Whole districts can be pillar halls (whose pillars run on into the next pillar hall), arched rooms, open offices of big rooms, or blackout.
  - A two-wide hallway runs along the inside of the wall where the level ends (and round the Red Rooms), so nothing the wall cuts through is left cut off.
- **Zones:**
  - Lobby;
  - open office, like the famous photo;
  - pillar halls;
  - arches (the Wikidot wiki's "pale walls with archway holes"): the same paper bleached pale, with the same baseboard, walls of arched openings over half-height walls, and an arch over every opening into the area; a wall between a pale area and a papered one is pale on one side and yellow on the other;
  - blackout areas: dark, silent, soaked carpet, flooded rooms and the odd sputtering light.
- **Rare rooms:**
  - pit rooms, a grid of 2×2 shafts 30 blocks deep (falling in lands you in Level 2);
  - the Manila Room: 8×8, wooden floor, a doorway on each side, and a table under a chandelier;
  - the Red Rooms: a block of rooms papered in red and lit by a few dim red lamps, walled off but for one narrow gap in each side. Stability drains there faster than almost anywhere, and the supply boxes inside are untouched.
- **Supplies:** two sealed supply boxes beside where you wake; elsewhere stashes of two or three boxes in the corners of rooms, about one cell in six (one in three or four in the blackout zones), and the caches at the hearts of dens. Over half are torn open already. Flint is rare in them (about one box in twenty): the Lobby's blades are glass and bracket, and flint waits in Level 1. Blackout zones are cold (10 °C).

## Level 1: the Habitable Zone

An endless cross between an underground car park and an abandoned warehouse.

- **Halls:** grey concrete laid out like a real multi-storey car park.
  - Columns every 9 blocks, with yellow-and-black striped feet, painted bay letters A–F and concrete beams under the slab. Some sectors have massive 2×2 columns.
  - Rows of 3-wide stalls with painted dividers, wheel stops and oil stains, back to back along the column lines.
  - 6-wide two-way aisles with dashed yellow centre lines, lane arrows and floor drains. Cross aisles every five bays pass under striped clearance bars.
  - A few abandoned cars (red, blue or white, sat down on flat tyres, the side glass gone and the windscreen crazed, rusting through; the black ones burnt out), crates and pallets left in stalls, shear walls carrying fire hose cabinets and power boxes, puddles with wet-floor signs and cones. Now and then a whole bay of three stalls has been used as a store: crates stacked in front of each wheel stop, a pallet, boxes or a supply box nearer the aisle.
  - Stairs to nowhere: now and then a short, two-wide concrete flight at the back of a stall climbs straight into the fire wall or shear wall behind it.
  - Traffic signs (ONE WAY, no entry, EXIT) stand about, pointing at walls and dead ends, and dead security cameras look down from the walls.
  - Concrete fire walls, scarred here and there to the rebar, divide the car park into districts 3 cells (96 blocks) square. They are open where every other driving aisle and every cross aisle runs through. The maintenance halls fill whole districts. Each district has its grid of bays to itself: its first row of stalls backs onto its north fire wall, a driving aisle runs along its south one, and cross aisles run along its west and east ones and down its middle, so a fire wall never cuts through a row of stalls or along an aisle.
  - **Stair cores** stand in the four corners of every district, by the fire walls: white-painted boxes with black fire doors propped open under a lit EXIT sign (one sign centred over the pair of doors). A pedestrian crossing is painted across the aisle from the doors of those backing onto a fire wall. Inside the doors is a landing under a bare bulb, with a fire standpipe and the floor painted on the wall in a disc (P5 to P1 in yellow, B1 to B3 in orange), and from it two flights run side by side between white walls, one up and one down, as from the landing of a real stair core: the flight up climbs through an opening in the slab and on up a shaft into the dark, the flight down descends through an opening in the floor and on under the wall into the dark.
  - **Landmarks:** wanderer groups tie coloured cloths with their emblem round columns and leave supplies at the foot.
- **Overhead:** twin-tube fluorescents on chains (whole sections dead or failing together), tube lights on columns, white conduit and red sprinkler mains under the slab.
- **Maintenance halls** fill whole sectors. They are entered through pairs of black fire doors, each pair under one lit EXIT sign centred over it.
  - Walls are white-painted block with a grey dado and a yellow stripe, much brighter than the car park.
  - Tight 2-wide corridors run in a grid, with pipes along one side (some joints drip), power boxes, breaker panels, outlets, fire alarms and extinguishers. There are crude drawings and old portraits on string and nails, loose wiring, hanging EXIT signs at crossings, and plywood and toolboxes against the walls. Their lights are on their own circuits, so whole stretches never go dead, though a Flickering still puts them out.
  - Between the corridors are small side rooms behind open doorways or locked steel doors. Most are empty and dark. Others are storerooms (shelving along the back and side walls, crates stacked in the corners, a rack down the middle of a big one, and the floor inside the door left clear), electrical rooms, workshops (workbench, pegboard, plywood), pump rooms with leaking pipes, first-aid rooms with hospital beds, little offices and break rooms, wanderers' camps, and stairwells (laid out like the stair cores, with a flight up, a flight down and a painted floor, in a room long enough for them). No corner of a room is fenced off by its furniture.
  - Some blocks are big, littered maintenance halls, stockrooms (rows of two-high racks with aisles between them, crates, a pallet and supply boxes at the aisles' blind ends), encampments of tents and bedrolls round a burning barrel (as big as the real things: two-person ridge tents two blocks by two, their backs to the wall, and sleeping bags two blocks long; where one has no room it is left out), or corridors cutting straight through between rows of rooms.
- **Supplies:** wooden crates, alone or stacked, are the best source of food and Almond Water.
- **Atmosphere:** grey fog, with a low ground fog hanging over the concrete. Humid, 28 °C.

## Level 2: Pipe Dreams

Dark concrete utility tunnels on a lattice. Three-wide main tunnels every 64 blocks run on for miles (now and then a length of main between two crossings is stepped 4–8 blocks aside, so it jogs at each end); between them, 1–2-wide service tunnels every 16 blocks form a maze grown in from the mains, with only a few ways in and many dead ends. Some crossings of two mains widen into a pump gallery. Each stretch of tunnel is one lighting circuit: all lit, all dead or all failing.

- **3D pipework:**
  - Bundles of cream lagged, rusty and red fire-line pipes run along the top of every tunnel and meet at junction boxes where tunnels turn or branch; where a run stops with open tunnel beyond it, its pipes end in blind flanges and caps. They sit above head height, so they never block the way.
  - Whole stretches of wall carry two continuous round pipes, rust-red steel over canvas-lagged, standing off the wall, with a flanged joint every four blocks and a strut and clamps between, and risers and red valve wheels along them. They follow the walls round corners (mitred round an outer corner, meeting the next wall's pipes in an inner one) and end in a blind flange where the stretch ends; other stretches are control cabinets with dial gauges, brick or hazard signs.
  - Here and there a cracked joint hisses and puffs steam.
- **Lights:** sparse buzzing fluorescent fixtures in white or orange, wired in series, so long sections are completely dark.
- **Tramway:** down some mains run the rusting rails of the B.N.T.G.'s narrow-gauge tramway, and now and then its little electric locomotive and a loaded goods wagon stand dead on them where the power left them, in the middle lane.
- **Doors:** now and then a steel door is set in a tunnel wall, painted white, grey, cream or grey-green. Most are locked. Some stand open on a small storeroom of shelving, boxes and a dead computer. A few, which look no different, open onto Level 3, Level 4 or Level 1.
- **Props:** machine cabinets line the pump galleries. Where tunnels widen there are oil drums (some still hold Almond Water), flatbed trolleys, toolboxes, wooden crates and cardboard boxes, and the pieces of dismantled machines lie against the walls. There is the rare forgotten supply box. Food is scarce.
- **Atmosphere:** a dark, murky haze. Hot, 38 °C.

## Level 3: the Electrical Station

A hot (35 °C), cramped maze of derelict brick and two-tone glazed-tile maintenance halls fitted with electrical machinery.

- **Layout:** corridors on a lattice, joined into one connected maze, deliberately inconsistent in size.
  - Tight districts have 2-wide lanes every 8 blocks; wide districts have galleries 4 wide and 4 high every 16.
  - Some links are 1-wide squeezes, half of them only two blocks high. Some stretches are floored with steel grating.
  - In each region of 5×5 cells, a 2×2-cell hall four blocks high may stand: a transformer hall or an assembly line. There is at most one Sanctum in each 8×8 region.
  - Many side routes are blocked by indestructible bars of cold blue-grey steel, so the way on is visible but out of reach. Some have a plaque beside them: OUT OF BOUNDS, DO NOT ENTER.
- **Machinery:** transformers, conductor coils, switchgear cabinets, industrial fans, generators and cable spools stand in niches lining the corridors, on hazard-striped floors.
  - The walls carry round pipe runs, cable bundles, pressure gauges, knife switches, junction boxes, DANGER HIGH VOLTAGE signs, alarm bells, caged bulkhead lamps and the odd sparking outlet.
  - Cable bundles in trays overhead droop in loops and loose ends. More cables trail across the floor.
  - The pipes overhead are black with the sludge they carry.
- **Lights:** sparse amber fixtures; whole stretches are dark.
- **Electrical rooms:** the solid blocks hold rooms of cinder block, each with a small sign beside its door bearing a number and a line of jumbled text.
  - Room types: machine rooms of switchgear and transformers, generator rooms, boiler rooms (with a pool of the scalding black sludge they make), control rooms (an operator's desk facing the meters), assembly lines whose conveyors still carry parcels, transformers caged behind bars, supply caches (this is a loot-rich level), freight elevators, and barred window rooms glowing with impossible daylight.
- **Rare:** a **Sanctum**: a hushed hall of pale masonry under a ribbed stone vault, with a red carpet between columns and candelabra, leading to a winged statue nearly three blocks tall on a dark pedestal, its halo glowing faintly.
- **Rarer:** something dead, half noclipped into a wall at the floor, it and the wall round it gone purple and glowing faintly: purpified.
- **Manholes:** these drop into flooded utility tunnels beneath the corridors, where the water is freezing (−4 °C). Ladder rungs lead back up.

## Level 4: the Abandoned Office

One endless, quiet, cool (15 °C) office floor, almost completely devoid of furniture.

- **Feel:** grey carpet with darker marks where desks once stood, scattered papers and coffee stains, drywall with a dark skirting.
  - The drop ceiling has fluorescent troffers (about half dead), air diffusers, sprinkler heads and water-stained tiles, and the odd tile is missing; now and then a cable hangs down out of the gap from a tray in the dark above.
- **Windows:** only in the wall where the office floor ends (the edge of the level). Most are blacked out or shut behind blinds. The few left clear show only dark, cold glass, and you are better off not looking out of them for long.
- **Walls:** evacuation maps, fire alarms with strobes, extinguisher cabinets, stopped clocks, light switches by the doors, wall phones, framed motivational posters, and the pale marks of pictures long taken down.
- **Open-plan floors:** columns on an 8-block grid with dead plants beside them (broken, a potted plant gives sticks, soil and now and then pottery shards, the makings of a slow clay), chairs and boxes left behind, pale rows where desks stood, and cubicle farms that run on from cell to cell, nearly all cleared out to bare partitions (a few patches untouched). Now and then a deserted reception.
- **Office blocks:** a corridor grid of small rooms, crossed every 48 blocks by a broad, bright concourse. Most offices are emptied out and some are locked. There are also glass conference rooms, break rooms (counter, sink, microwave, fridge), copy rooms, humming server rooms, storerooms, a manager's office and restrooms.
- **Building cores:** stairwells under EXIT signs (a stairwell takes in the corridor beside it, and has a flight up through the ceiling and a flight down through the floor, side by side from a landing inside its door, like Level 1's) and elevator lobbies with two-wide lift doors stand together with a restroom round one crossing, about one core per 2×2 cells of offices.
- **Refuges:** the outposts of those who rest here, each round a trading wanderer: bedrolls on the carpet, a camp lantern, a folding table and chair, boxes of what they have gathered, a crate of supplies, now and then a tent, out on the open floor where there is room. The bedrolls are sleeping bags two blocks long, heads to either side of the lantern, and the tent a ridge tent two blocks by two in the corner behind them.
- **Almond Water:** drinking fountains, water coolers and vending machines still give it. Use them to take a bottle; each has a few left.
  - A few vending machines give nothing. They take whoever presses their buttons to the Snackrooms, every time, and always to the same food court.

## Level 5: the Terror Hotel

A 1930s grand hotel that never ends (20 °C), oddly clean and well kept.

- **Corridors:** long, 3-wide, double-loaded corridors (rooms on both sides), every 16 blocks. Now and then one does not carry on across a cross corridor but ends in a tall mirror.
  - Red-and-gold damask carpet, or walnut boards under a runner.
  - Mahogany wainscot under red panels in gilt frames, candle sconces and dome lights.
  - On the walls: portraits, gilt mirrors, landscapes, house phones, fire hose cabinets, clocks, and brass speakers playing jazz from nowhere.
  - Along the walls: console tables with lamps and dried flowers, bookshelves, curio cabinets, sofas and armchairs, radiators, luggage carts, room-service trolleys, laundry carts and ice machines.
  - Room-service trays left on the carpet outside doors, the plates still covered.
- **Elevators** are rare. Each is two blocks wide in a cross-corridor wall, with a call plate beside it. Most are antique copper cages under a floor dial; a few are modern lifts.
- **Guest rooms:** five blocks square, most with a toilet in an alcove by the door. Numbered doors with gold plaques, odd on one side of the corridor and even on the other, counting up 201, 203, 205, 207 and then, a few doors on, starting again; more than half locked. One locked door in three hundred reads 666. A ceiling fan turns over each furnished room. Open rooms are often fully furnished: the bed's head against the back wall, nightstands with lamps, a wardrobe, a writing desk with a green banker's lamp, an armchair or floor lamp, a suitcase, and a rug on bare boards. Now and then a turntable plays jazz.
- **Grand lobbies:** a taller coffered ceiling on mahogany beams, red faux-marble columns over white marble, and chandeliers.
  - Seating groups round coffee tables, a front desk with a guest book and bell, a grandfather clock, palms, luggage carts and bookshelves.
  - **Front halls** (one in about every four by four cells of lobby): the end of the hall closed by a panelled wall between its columns, the front desk on the hall's axis with a key rack (brass hooks and room keys) on the wall behind it, a lamp at either end and the clock; a red runner up the axis to the desk under the chandeliers; and either side of the desk a grand staircase, white marble with a runner and brass stair rods, climbing between marble walls up through the ceiling into the dark. Climb one and you come out in the Abandoned Office, at the top of a stairwell's flight down; that flight leads back down to it.
- **Dining halls:** round tables laid with china and candles for a dinner nobody comes to, chairs drawn up, chandeliers, a grand piano.
- **The Beverly Room:** a ballroom lined with doors on every side, most locked, a few opening onto somewhere else. On the small table under its great chandelier are a half-finished game of Mahjong and drinks nobody has touched. Placards stand beside its four real doors; the plates on its other doors are blank (they are no guest rooms' doors).
- **Boiler halls:** STAFF ONLY doors (a black and brass plate over the doorway, centred over it however wide it is) lead into hot (42 °C) halls of stained plaster crowded with pipes, valves and gauges. Barred machinery bays hold boilers, glowing furnaces and coal, under pipes and steam leaks.
  - The furnaces burn and smoke, the boilers steam and give off heat, and pressure tanks, gauge boards and damper levers fill the bays. Sumps of scalding water lie against some back walls.
- **Candles really burn:** in the sconces, on the dining tables and in the chandeliers.

## Level 6: Lights Out

Long grey concrete corridors (8 °C) in darkness that light barely reaches: a candle shows the floor at your feet, a torch a few steps, a lantern a stretch of corridor. Now and then the dark lifts for a heartbeat. Things scuttle, breathe and whisper out of sight. Stability is low and drifts.

- **Layout:** trunk corridors on a 64-block lattice, two wide; every fourth is a main trunk, three wide and never broken. Between the mains the minor trunks are a maze with one way in, so most wrong turns end blind a hundred blocks on. Narrow side passages, wet underfoot, wind a little way into the rock off every trunk and stop. Some crossings open into a chamber, deeper in often with stairs down.
- **Finding the stairs:** the faint sound of waves comes from the way the nearest stairs lie (within 160 blocks), louder as you close in, and chalk arrows on the trunk walls point toward them (from up to 400 blocks off in worlds made from 0.514.0, 220 before).
- **Navigation fails:** the map only records what is within arm's reach. Chalk marks are the one thing that stays where you left it.
- **M.E.G. markings:** earlier explorers left arrows to the exits, crosses at dead ends, handprints and scratches on the walls.
- **A light switch** turns up on a wall now and then. It does nothing.
- **Chambers** open off the corridors. Deep in the level, a few hold stairs down toward the sound of waves: about one chamber in a hundred near Level 5, one in sixteen halfway through the level and one in five at its far edge, some ten times rarer than before 0.514.0. The stairs are a stairwell two blocks wide, eight steps down through the rock with headroom all the way to a landing eight blocks below the floor. Walk on across the landing and you come out at the foot of the stairs into one of Level 7's entry rooms; climb those stairs and you are back on the landing. (In worlds made before 0.514.0 they are three steps in the middle of the chamber, about one chamber in two has them, and they drop you into the sea, one way.)
- **Tripwires** strung low across narrow, straight side passages drop you into the Snackrooms.
- **What others left:** at some dead ends lie a dead flashlight, a backpack or a bedroll (a sleeping bag two blocks long, laid out along the passage). Water stands on the floor in places.

## Level 6.1: the Snackrooms

A rare, sealed food court inside Level 6 (21 °C), bright and safe, with almost the highest stability anywhere (only the Manila Room's is higher).

- Restaurants with counters, registers, stoves and fryers; convenience stores with shelves and glass-fronted drinks fridges; seating areas with café tables, ficus trees and vending machines; a mall map.
- In about one food court in two, a shop "unenterable due to the sheer amount of vending machines piled on top of each other": dead machines standing, toppled and lying across each other's tops, wall to wall behind its windows. Only the one in the doorway still works; the rest salvage for brackets, glass, wire and the odd snack.
- Vending machines and drink fridges give chips, candy bars, crackers, soda, bottled water, Almond Water and cashew water, and restock once a week. Shelves, stoves and fryers can be salvaged.
- Getting in is unreliable, and so is getting out: every exit leads somewhere different, and only the kitchen door you came in by from a city diner leads reliably back.

## Level 7: Thalassophobia

An endless sea under a concrete ceiling (12 °C, 6 °C below the surface). It gets deeper and darker the further out you go: shallows near the walls, then the twilight zone, the midnight zone and the abyss, with trenches and the odd rocky island.

- **The ceiling and the light** (worlds made from 0.514.0): the lore's "high concrete ceiling suspended above the water", thirty-six blocks up, as high as the world allows, with its faint glowing patches far overhead. The level has no lamps but a "dim natural light" from nowhere, the same at every hour (no day and night), that fades as you dive until the midnight zone is dark. A thin grey sea haze hides the far distance. (In worlds made before 0.514.0 the ceiling is five blocks over the water and the sea is dark but for the ceiling's glowing patches.)
- **Entry rooms** (worlds made from 0.514.0) hang from the ceiling in piers of concrete, five blocks above the water: a forgotten study with a bookcase, a coffee table, a chair and a fluorescent lamp, its carpet soaked. The stairs from Level 6 come down into it, and you arrive at their foot; climb them back up and you are on the landing of the stairwell you came down. Across the room from the stairs a doorway opens onto the sea, and a rope ladder hangs from it down into the water: the way back up from the sea. (In worlds made before 0.514.0 the entry rooms are set into the low ceiling and you drop through a hole in the floor into the sea.)
- **Air under the sea** (worlds made from 0.514.0): out over the twilight and midnight zones, about one stretch of sea in ten has a buoy with a rope ladder hanging from it all the way to the bottom. Under it lies a room on the seabed with air still trapped under its roof, its fluorescent light still on and showing through a window in each side. Climb down the line, swim in through the low tunnel in its foot beside the line, and come up through the hatch in its floor: there is air to breathe there before you climb back up. Each has a chest whose loot is better the deeper it lies: a **sea chest** in the twilight zone (rope, bandages, candles, chalk, flint, copper bits and scrap wire, food and Almond Water, now and then a copper knife or pickaxe, a rusty or a temporal gear, a note) and a supply box; a **sunken strongbox** in the midnight zone (copper and tin bronze ingots, silver and gold bits, a bronze pickaxe or knife or a copper prospecting pick now and then, rusty gears and, one in seven, a temporal gear) and a lost backpack. Wreck crates, scrap, bones and packs lie round them on the seabed. The deep ones are where the Thing circles, and the light inside a midnight room is too deep to light it: bring your own. While you breathe in one, the Thing waits outside. A wanderer's note in Level 7 may mark the nearest one. In any world, every cache on Level 7's seabed (wreck crates, packs, boxes, the chests) pays more the deeper it lies, the bottom of the midnight zone as much as the city.
- **The seabed** holds bones, scrap metal, skeletons, lost backpacks and wreck crates to salvage, and pools of scalding tar.
- **Stability** falls the deeper you dive.
- **Islands** rise in steps from the water's edge, and pillars of their dark rock stand up into the ceiling, from the larger islands and as sea stacks out of the shallows and the twilight zone: the landmarks you steer by. The ceiling's faint glow lies in irregular patches.
- **Camps** stand on some of the islands, after the manner of Fort Surrender: tents, bedrolls, a lantern, a supply box. One camp in two has a shelter like the fort's own, "composed of broken furniture held together by tar": a ring of cabinets, bookcases and chairs, some stuck down on lumps of tar, round a bedroll. The tents are two-person ridge tents two blocks by two and the bedrolls sleeping bags two blocks long, on one step of the island, wherever they fit.
- **Old jetties** run out from the ledges over the water and simply stop. Driftwood and buoys float in the shallows; kelp rises from the twilight zone.
- **Things grow** where the water is shallow: seagrass, seaweed and wrack sway round the islands and ledges. Between the kelp of the twilight zone stands a dead reef, bleached coral and nothing else; the midnight zone and the abyss are bare. Shells and boulders lie on the island rims.
- Saltwater fish swim here. Boats and fishing work as in vanilla.

## Level 8: Cave Systems

Real Vintage Story geology (9 °C): strata of real rock with ores, flooded pockets, stalactites, tar pools, and strata that are wrong, such as office carpet layers and vertical coal seams. Wretches roam the dark.

- **Almond Water** seeps out of the rock beside the road in places, trickling down a wet stain into a pool on the floor. Use a seep to fill a bottle; it comes back.
- **The 9th Road:** a gravel road through the caves, lit by lamps and marked by M.E.G. waypoints, with camps along it. The road is rare and the caves go on for ever: two long roads, a few hundred blocks apart, wander the length of the level, bending round rich mineral deposits (solid rock thick with one ore) and now and then splitting round one and meeting again. Every few hundred blocks a way runs off a road through the level's wall into Level 7 or Level 9, or across to the other road; camps stand where such a way meets a road, at bends, beside the deposits and at the end of short spurs. (In worlds made before 0.514.0 the road is a grid instead: main roads every 192 blocks run unbroken, the side roads between them a maze of dead ends, with camps at the crossings.) Stability is high on the road and lower off it. Where it reaches another level, it runs on through the wall. It is a rough arch (three blocks high at the sides, four to six in the middle, wherever a cave does not open over it), its lamps hang out over the road, and its camps are domed chambers, nine blocks high in the middle. The camps' tents are two-person ridge tents two blocks by two and their bedrolls sleeping bags two blocks long.
- **Ore** lies in veins and pockets, in few, rich blocks.
- **In the caves:** cobwebs under the ceilings, quartz and amethyst growing from the rock, mushrooms and loose stones, and now and then the remains of somebody who went off the road.
  - Tapered stalactites and stalagmites in the rock they grow from, in groves, always leaving room to walk round.
  - Hanging lichen, bracket fungi on the walls, cave-ins of boulders, and big crystal clusters deep down.
  - In a few tall chambers, a colony of glowworms: the only light the caves make themselves; also glowing mushrooms in some stretches of cave and along the foot of the road's walls, mostly blood-red, now and then of many colours.

## Level 9: the Suburbs

An endless neighbourhood at midnight (14 °C) under an open sky that never gets light, a thin black cloud drifting over the stars.

- **The streets:** two-lane roads with a dashed centre line, zebra crossings and stop signs at the junctions, manhole covers, puddles and drifts of dead leaves, fire hydrants, street lamps (mostly dead), wheelie bins at the kerb and cars parked along it. A green street-name sign on a corner of every junction (the same sixteen names over and over), and at one junction in six a DOWNTOWN sign with an arrow toward the city.
- **The houses** differ: siding in white, blue, yellow or green, or brick, with a chimney; a porch with chairs or a garage with a drive and a basketball hoop; flower beds and shrubs out front, sometimes a white picket fence. The windows are framed and glazed, with a sill. About a third have two storeys (a hall with the stairs up it, three bedrooms and a bathroom upstairs), nearly half have a porch on posts with a deck and steps, the windows come in pairs, every other block or every third, and a coach lamp hangs by every front door, lit in the houses that still have power.
- **In worlds made from 0.514.0** the houses look like real ones: tall sash windows (shuttered on the front of a two-storey house and some others), a picture window in the living room, a two-storey house's roof over both its storeys, some gables facing the street, some garages under a lower roof of their own with the door cased in trim, brick chimneys capped with a flue pot (up a gable end or through the ridge). Front and back doors are real doors: right-click to open or shut them (some are found open). A locked house's front door stays shut until you force it with a pry bar. The stairs are open overhead all the way up, with a balustrade on the open side.
- **Houses are dungeons:** living rooms with a sofa and a TV that doesn't work, dining tables, kitchens with cupboards and pantries to search, bathrooms, a main bedroom and sometimes a child's room with a toy box, a laundry, garages with tools and sometimes a car. Family photos, paintings, clocks and mirrors hang on the walls; the ceiling lights are dead. Some houses are locked.
- **Backyards** are fenced (from 0.514.0 neighbours share one fence): a patio with a grill, and a swing set, a vegetable patch, a garden shed, a dog house or a drained pool.
- **Nobody mows:** real grass sways in the night wind, short on the front lawns, long and weedy in the backyards and empty lots, tallest against the fences. Ferns and fallen sticks lie under the trees: oak, birch and maple with round crowns.
- Raccoons, foxes and hares roam at night.
- Behind some houses, a path through a field leads to a gate.

## Level 10: Bumper Crop

Endless farmland under a leaden overcast that hides the sun (18 °C), and one of the safest places in the Backrooms. Now and then, for a few minutes, a spell of thick fog comes over the fields and lifts again (the lore's light rain is left out: Vintage Story's rain falls by region, not by level, and wets whatever is under it).

- **The fields are vast**, some two hundred blocks across and irregular: spelt, rye and flax with furrows and tractor tramlines running to the horizon, a scarecrow keeping watch and, further out, a lone grey tool shed, a galvanised water trough with a hand pump, or a field gate fallen flat in a trampled patch; stubble fields with round bales left in rows; meadows of long grass and wildflowers. Hedgerows with the odd oak, or strips of rough grass, divide one field from the next.
- **Farm roads** wander for kilometres, two bare tire tracks with a strip of short grass between, lined with telegraph poles that carry no wires.
- **Farmsteads** stand alone: a farmhouse, a red barn with hay and stalls, a grain silo, a tool shed, an old tractor and plough in the yard. Fenced paddocks hold livestock; ponds lie in hollows ringed with reeds, sedge and water lilies.
- **The grass moves:** meadows of long grass in the wind, a wet, deep green rather than the yellow-green of the Suburbs' lawns, poppies, cornflowers and lupins along the field margins, cropped grass in the paddocks.
- **The soil is thin:** a metre down is a mass of worms. Dig into it and they erupt, biting and killing the crops around.
- **Some barns are thin too:** in some, a stretch of the east wall between the stalls isn't quite there. Its boards look slightly out of true, and dust hangs in front of it. Walk into it and you noclip into Level 11.

## Level 11: the Endless City

A Western metropolis that goes on forever (17 °C), with its own day and night under a thin haze, and the safest, best-connected place in the Backrooms. It is not a ring: it lies some fifty kilometres out past the fields, behind a wall of black glass that no one has been beyond, and from the rings the only way in is to noclip (the Hub and Level Fun have doors of their own into it). Its streets lie 48 blocks below the other levels' floor, so its towers can rise almost to the top of the world.

- **The grid:** an avenue every hundred blocks and a street every seventy-six, so every block is the same size, cut in two by an alley. Many blocks look alike, and some are identical at a glance.
- **Districts:** downtown, always near the middle and elsewhere in patches, is all towers, up to 80 blocks high, of curtain glass, limestone or concrete, stepping back as they rise; some stand alone on granite plazas. Midtown is apartment blocks over stores, offices and hotels. Further out the buildings are mostly brick, three or four storeys at most: rowhouses behind railed yards, corner stores, churches, garages and substations.
- **Over a quarter of the buildings downtown, and fewer further out, can never be entered.** Their windows are dull black mirrors and they have no doors.
- **The rest have their lights on and their shelves stocked:**
  - office lobbies (reception desk, benches, ficus, stairs that climb into the ceiling and stop);
  - hotel lobbies under a chandelier;
  - apartment halls with mailboxes, a directory and a missing-person poster;
  - diners, grocery stores and bodegas, pharmacies (and farmacias), hardware stores, clothing stores and laundromats (a store with a pair of doors has a sign twice as wide centred over them);
  - churches with pews and candles, a museum whose galleries hang paintings of the other levels, parking garages with cars on every deck.

  The floors above the ground floor are dark behind their windows, and nobody has been up to them.
- **The streets are empty:**
  - cars are parked along every curb;
  - the traffic lights are dead, stuck on red or blinking amber, and most street lamps are out;
  - there are bus shelters, newspaper boxes, parking meters, phone booths, hydrants and mailboxes;
  - round advertising pillars pasted with posters stand on some corners, and billboards on legs stand on some of the low brick roofs, their posters to the street (Almond Water, the diner, "the city that never sleeps", one stripped to shreds);
  - street signs are in English and Spanish;
  - the only sound is a hum like distant traffic that isn't there.
- **Anomalies:** now and then a stretch of sidewalk has come loose and runs on up into the sky.
- **Almond Water is everywhere:** in vending machines, on street vendors' carts (nobody is minding them), in the fridges of every grocery.
- **The subway** runs under every avenue, two tracks in a tiled tunnel, with a station at every third street: platforms, benches, maps, an Almond Water machine, and stairs up to both sidewalks at both ends.
- **Parks:**
  - lawns behind iron railings, gravel paths, trees, benches and lamps;
  - a statue among flowers and a playground;
  - a fountain where the paths meet;
  - in some, a corner of wheat that nobody planted.
- **Scavenging:**
  - Grocery and pharmacy shelves hold canned food, flour and salt, and bandages.
  - Hardware stores hold copper tools, nails, rope and wire. Clothing racks and wardrobes hold clothes.
  - Dumpsters and bags of rubbish in the alleys give paper, rotten wood, glass and bones.
  - Parking meters give copper and silver bits.
- **The ways out are as many as they are hard to notice:** see the table above.

## Level 37: Sublimity, the Poolrooms

An expansive complex of rooms and corridors of white ceramic tile, flooded with lukewarm water (29 °C). It is not a ring: it lies far out to the west, and from the rings you get there through water or a door marked POOL (the Hub's tiled tunnel ends lead there too). It follows the lore of the Wikidot page by egglord, with Fandom and Miraheze as second opinions.

- **The tile** is a mosaic of small white squares (about 12 cm) in thin pale grout, glazed, on every surface; the edge of a deck where it drops into deep water is laid with coping. Rooms are rounded at their corners, as in the renders that defined the place.
- **Strict in design, senseless in purpose:** wandering flooded galleries every 96 blocks, with far too many pillars down their middles and a handrail along one wall; between them, a grid of rooms, all different, joined by halls on a lattice and by openings that make no sense:
  - domed rotundas round a circular pool, ringed by arched openings glowing white, with pool ladders;
  - natatoriums far too big to be pools: a shallow end, steps, and a deep end with no ledge to climb out onto;
  - wading halls in a forest of pillars; pools with a deck round them; basins stepping down to a deep middle; halls of water too deep to wade;
  - tunnels of tiled arch rings over shallow water, a step running along one side, ending in a black round mouth or in deep water going nowhere;
  - stair pits: broad steps going straight down into water too deep and dark to see the bottom of;
  - serpentine halls, a channel of water winding through in long curves, slits down the outside of each bend;
  - halls with a whole wall of slits; now and then a long dry corridor whose tile gives way, at its far end, to office carpet;
  - here and there a swim through a hole under a wall, surfacing through a sump in a gallery's floor.
- **Light:** no lamps. Tall slits set back into the walls glow with glare from somewhere you can never see, laying warm stripes across the tile; about a third of the floor is out of their reach, in the dark.
- **The water heals:** standing in it in the light mends 0.5 HP every 4 seconds, steadies you and counts as rest. Nothing hostile ever comes (a sanctuary everywhere but the Reservoir). There is no food at all.
- **Stability:** 1.35 in the light, 0.6 in the dark corners, 0.15 in the Reservoir.
- **What there is:** crusts of Epsom salt on the rims in the dark (2 Epsom salt above 1 linen = 3 of the best poultice); lost towels, flip-flops and pool noodles; at the bottom of the stair pits, what the drowned left.
- **Sound:** the soft wash of water, a drip or a lap now and then, and in the dark corners the water swelling until it drowns everything out.

### Level 37.1: the Sanguine Reservoir

One block in twenty, far out in the Poolrooms: the slits stop, the water stands deeper and red and holds on to whoever wades in it (half pace), and bright red sprouts on the pool floors are all the light there is. A chanting is heard from the way the nearest Reservoir lies, up to 160 blocks off. Smilers are the only thing ever seen there. On the bottom lie the people who stayed, and their gear, untouched. A sprout's red water heals every wound, and lowers for good how steady you can ever be (5% each drink, never below half).

## Level 94: Motion

A stop-motion picture of the 1930s to 50s that never stops being made: round green hills mown in stripes, each with one cream house on top; winding roads in the valleys with a STOP sign at the junctions and a NO OUTLET sign at roads that go on forever; white picket fences, white water towers full of Almond Water, old cars parked in the middle of nowhere. It lies far out to the north, and everyone who comes falls into it out of the sky and lands softly. Its edge is the painted backdrop of the set.

- **The hills** are domes on a jittered lattice 56 blocks apart, mown in stripes, never steeper than a block a block. Each house stands level on its hilltop, its yard falling away to the hill, a path (in steps) down to the nearest road. The houses are two storeys of cream-yellow clapboard (in worlds made before 0.514.0 some are white) under red or brown shingles, with flower beds and white picket fences; inside they are furnished in the 1950s: iceboxes, radio cabinets, telephones on little tables.
- **The grass is real:** short grass, as green as the turf, grows on the hills in the mown stripes, thicker on the dark ones, and it sways a little, slowly. Long grass and bright flowers grow along the fences and verges and round the water towers' legs.
- **The ground is clay:** the turf can be dug (poor soil), and the red modelling clay under it (clay for the potter); roads, paths and anything built only break down to rubble, with a pickaxe. The edge of the set is sealed underground.
- **Towns** in some valleys: eight houses round a paved plaza with a fountain of Almond Water, siren poles at its corners, milk vans full of Almond Milk at the kerb.
- **The gradient:** the nearer the middle, the more the houses come apart: a kitchen alone on a hill, then a bedroom that is one wall and a floor, then a living room with no room, then heaps of furniture, a car tipped on its side, a staircase climbing to nothing. Follow it to the glass mountain.
- **Day and night:** by day the siren poles play the same happy tune and nothing hunts. In the last hours of the afternoon the tune slows and goes flat; at dusk it stops, laughter comes from over the hills, and the Animations come out. Indoors (under a roof) is safe; out of doors, whoever stands quite still for four seconds is taken for a doll.
- **Plenty to eat:** iceboxes in every house, Almond Milk in the milk vans (restocked weekly), Almond Water from the fountains and the towers, real clay a block under the turf.
- **The glass mountain and the castle:** a cone of glass 40 blocks high in the middle, a white castle with red-roofed towers on its flat top. Its walls and roofs glow a little, so it still stands out at dusk, and it is on the horizon from anywhere in the hills: past the view distance a picture of it is drawn where it would be. Inside nothing is animated: a funhouse of checkerboard floors and striped walls with porthole rings, a ball pit sunk into the floor (you wade through it), two blinding halls all in white, the animation studio (the model of the town on its table), an arcade, slides. Robo-Men stand guard: touch, use or break anything in the castle and they come for a minute and a half.
- **The King:** the throne room, a dim old dining hall of dark panelling and deep red carpet under two chandeliers, has a door on each side; only one opens, a different one each day. Sit in the second chair at his table and he raises your nightmare (the kind of thing that has killed you most often) to fight you, at one and a half times its health; its blows are a quarter as strong when your stability is full and twice as strong when it is gone. Win, and he bursts into light: the first time you get a shard of his crown (while you carry it the Animations take you for one of their own), and you are carried to Level 1, Level 4 or back to a town.

## The seven far places

Seven more regions lie beyond every ring, like the city, the Poolrooms and Motion, reached only by thresholds (see the table above): Level ! to the south, Level Fun to the north-east, The End to the north-west, the Hub to the south-east, Level 974 to the south-west, Level 3999 far to the south-east beyond the Hub, and Level 188 north-north-east, between Motion and Level Fun.

### Level !: Run For Your Life!

One long hospital hallway, lit only by red EXIT signs, with an alarm blaring.

- **A lane for each runner:** 17 copies of the hall side by side, each about 480 blocks of running (about 70 seconds at the game's sprint of 6.8 blocks a second; 615 blocks in worlds made before 0.514.0), with doglegs, two false exits (a door with a turn beside it, under a darker EXIT sign whose X has burnt out, dark underneath, opening on a closet), in one lane in five the Safe Zone, and in one in three a hole into Level 8. The true exit is at a dead end with no turn either side, under a bright sign, with a faint light coming from under it (a false exit has the same gap, dark); each lane's leads to Level 1 (9 lanes), Level 4 (5) or The End (3).
- **The chase:** a start room in red light (a hospital room's walls: crash rail, visiting hours, a hand-washing notice and a sanitiser dispenser), a chained door that bursts, the hall door that sticks (the way out, under the room's lit EXIT sign; it opens on the third pull, and every other door there says which one to take); the alarm muffled in the walls from the moment you arrive, deafening once the door bursts; then the horde's front runs at four fifths of the runner's own sprint (a little slower for its first 20 seconds), falling back out of sight while they sprint, and catching (killing) whoever comes within a block and a half. What is seen of it are the Backrooms' own entities puppeted along behind the front.
- **Hazards, all announced:** side doors flying open, shelving toppling, dented doors bursting, a vent dropping a Hound, an EXIT sign bursting into a wall of fire, heaps of junk on the floor (toys, boxes, tangled cords, fallen ceiling) that trip a sprinter who runs over them instead of jumping.
- **What is in the way** (packed: a set piece every few blocks): barricades of beds and chairs, tall supply cabinets with a door hanging open, and collapsed ceiling (a slab hanging from the hole onto rubble, live wires over it) to go round; gurneys (along, across or tipped on their sides), wheelchairs, IV stands, chairs, crash carts, fallen shelving and heaps of rubble to jump (a sprint-jump clears a block); lines of them across the hall with one gap, needles a block wide, and feints that open along a wall and end. Every straight is kept passable at a sprint (a block wide, two high, a column across per block forward, jumping a block).
- **The Safe Zone:** a blue stretch of hall and its side rooms; nothing comes in for three in-game hours (the walls shudder at two), not the horde nor what burst out of a door behind you. **Energy Bars** (bedside table, trays, Safe Zone shelves): food, and a second wind of +12% speed for 30 seconds.
- Escape once and every way in takes you elsewhere in your own level instead. A red "!" door is left where you came out.

### Level Fun =)

Endless party rooms on a ship that cannot be this big (22 °C).

- **Layout:** 96-block plans of promenades and halls, elevator lobbies where they cross (people arrive only there; dressed for the party too: balloons, streamers, string lights over the doors, confetti, a TIME 4 FUN =) poster, the ship's directory and a potted palm, one pair of doors OUT OF ORDER =)), and in between party suites, themed rooms (movie night, Halloween, tea party), ball pits, bounce halls, arcades, kitchens, gift rooms, crew corridors, open decks over a black sea, waiting rooms. One block in eight has a cloned hallway. Everything is furnished in 3D: tables, cakes, plates, gifts, balloons, streamers, HAPPY BIRTHDAY bunting.
- **Partygoers:** guests round the head table of rooms with a balloon arch and music, nearsighted and slow, joining in by sight. Their latch is Party Fever unless treated in 30 s; full fever kills you and a new Partygoer guards your things. A party hat and a red balloon disguise you beyond 3 blocks. Carnivorous Balloons wait in the dark.
- **Food:** birthday cake by the slice, cookies, pumpkins, gifts (one in five a popper); Red Velvet cake feeds you and gives you fever. Wanderers' notes here are altered: "=)" means a lie.

### The End

A library that goes on forever under THE END IS NEAR, the cruellest trap: it looks like the way out (19 °C).

- **Layout:** 64-block departments on a wandering runway round a central hall: stacks, open floor, service desks, reading rooms, two-storey atriums, stockrooms with EXIT doors, collapses, long aisles, and six fake realities (the Lobby, an office, a hotel corridor, a house, a snowfield, Home) that the shelving blends into. Along about one department border in three, a stacks or open-floor department has the store's painted wall (yellow band, ghost marks, posters), broken by openings every 8 to 12 blocks; the central hall's EXIT doors stand at the back of little vestibules.
- **Mechanics:** stability 1.2 in the central hall, falling outward; after two in-game hours an isolation drain; lights out round you now and then; distortions (12 s of another place's fog and sound; walk into a wall during one and you go there); one-use computers that mark the nearest open exits (one in four says "=)" and takes you to Level Fun); fragile shelves.
- **Finds:** vanilla chairs, tables, ladders and shelves, paper, books (some written in), catalogue cards that mark the way out. The Junkies, wanderers in the Home fake realities, trade only with someone carrying Memory Juice.

### The Hub

A hidden crossroads of road tunnels under amber lamps, silent, cold (6 °C) and safe.

- **Layout:** ten numbered spokes from a roundabout round the Tower, five ring roads, and a lattice of side tunnels, all lined with carved concrete doors under crop-circle sigils (fixed per level in every world). About 15,500 are dead; 20 lead somewhere (and one window climbs into Level 188): Levels 1, 11 and the Snackrooms ("178") open for anyone, Level 974's pink ribbon door for someone carrying something cute, the rest only to their **Level Key**, which is used up. Every carved door is one-way. Grey service doors are the ways back.
- **Level Keys:** found only in their own level (dens 4%, forced rooms 1.5%, worst places 8%, otherwise 0.2% of caches broken; Level !'s runners' remains, The End's desks and catalogues, Level Fun's gifts 4%), sold by the M.E.G. outpost's wanderer (two at a time, 30–45 rusty gears), or given by the Keymaster. A held key chimes from its door's direction, and you are told when you come near its door.
- **The Tower:** a rotunda with a headless statue, a spiral stair past four galleries of forbidden doors to a meeting hall and a balcony over a black void. You are let in once. The Keymaster gives one key of a level you have been to, then escorts you to its door. Break anything of the Tower's own and he throws you out with nothing; a torch you set down to see by is yours to take up again. (An admin, or you in singleplayer, can grant another visit with `/backrooms hub reset [player]`.)
- **The Far Tunnels:** past the third ring road the lamps die out; a false wall to Level 4, holes to Level 94, tiled ends to Level 37.
- Now and then, far from home, a door into the Hub stands in a wall for five minutes (the manifest door).

### Level 974: Kitty's House

A small pink house with no outside, one for each player, where Kitty takes whoever carries something cute.

- **Getting in:** Kitty's detour on any threshold crossing (1 in 20 with something cute on you, 1 in 6 if held; 48 in-game hours apart), or the Hub's pink door. Cute: plush toys (Level 9's toy boxes, now and then suitcases and lost backpacks), vanilla flowers and seashells, Kitty's dresser clothes.
- **The house:** seven rooms, painted windows, a real bed, taps that fill buckets, a fridge and pantry that refill (turning to sweets after 24 and 48 hours), remembered meals, an old computer (e-mail, a map mark of where you will wake, DreamSleep). A sanctuary, stability 1.3, with an overstay drain from 18 hours.
- **Kitty:** appears where you are not looking, glides out of your way, watches you sleep, never harms. Breaking its things or striking it puts the lights out and empties the kitchen for a day. Give it the cute thing you brought and you wake in a safe place fixed per house (Level 4's heart, Level 37 or Level 6.1), watched over for three minutes.

### Level 3999: The Neon Paradise

An endless arcade-mall under neon, safe at its heart and dark at its fringe: an ordinary level with resources of its own (24 °C).

- **Layout:** 128-block plan blocks; concourses 12 wide and 18 high with galleries, balustrades, two opposite moving walkways (2.5 blocks a second), neon arches and bridges; junctions under a giant "3999" sign, with directories and escalators; service corridors; units of game areas (rows of one kind of machine, a prize counter, now and then a mini arcade with its elevator), game halls and food courts (gallery rings, five to seven stalls), lobbies (reception opposite a revolving door, a storey line of black marble, a row of machines on the back wall), bowling lanes, shuttered stores, and past 30% of the radius the violet-pooled Neon Natatorium (34 °C; its water heals half a point every four seconds). The rim looks onto a lit car park through painted windows. Arrivals are on lobby floors.
- **Tickets and prizes:** hold right-click to play a machine; each pays a player once per in-game day (cabinets 0–3, pinball 0–4, racers, dance and skee-ball 1–5, a 3% jackpot of 12; the claw a plush one go in four), at most 40 tickets a day. Prize counters give one prize of the dearest tier the held tickets afford (5, 15, 40, 200: candy and plushes, seeds and neon lamps, a lantern or pry bar, a temporal gear); food stalls a tray meal for 6; vending machines a snack or soda for one Almond Water, never empty. Machines, counters, doors and windows are indestructible.
- **Resources:** popcorn, cotton candy, Paradise Pop, tray meals; salvage from dead cabinets, air hockey, bins, fallen ceiling and dead neon: neon tubing, circuit boards (melt to copper, 10 an ingot), coin mechanisms (brass, 5 an ingot), plastic scrap (fuel); neon lamps (light 9) and glow bracelets, as prizes and by recipe. Plushes are cute (Level 974).
- **Safety:** stability 1.3 (lobbies 1.4, the Natatorium 1.35), a sanctuary, wanderers' camps in one lobby in four (the Megacolony). **Level 3999.1, the Neon Blackout:** one plan block in 14 past 65% of the radius, inside 48 blocks of failing neon: 14 °C, stability 0.15, the Threshold band with Smilers, Hounds and Partygoers; free prize cases and ticket piles.
- **Arcade Attendants** (plum or navy): behind the counters, stalls and desks while anyone is near; invulnerable; right-click for the way to a lobby (at a lobby's reception desk, the way to the nearest food court). Mall directories mark the nearest food court and lobby.
- **Light:** neon tubes (light 16 under the ceiling and galleries, and every six blocks across the lobbies' and food courts' ceilings), pillars (15, every ten blocks in lobbies and among the food courts' tables), wall tubes (13), base strips (12), signs (11–14): floors average about 10 in lobbies and food courts.

### Level 188: The Courtyard of Windows

One shared building: a dark courtyard of mossy lawns and a round wooden deck, walled in by a five-storey hotel with 160 courtyard windows, under a black sky.

- **The building** (88 × 58 blocks): five storeys of four blocks, a scissor stair to the roof (north-west corner), 32 courtyard windows a storey, inner and outer rooms furnished from one plan, corner suites, the lobby (locked front door, breakfast dispensers), dead lifts, housekeeping and the linen store, the roof with parapets and AC units; the courtyard's lawns, deck, hatch, benches, bollards and drain, and its one door (to the stair) under a green EXIT sign.
- **Windows:** each window's state is a function of the world and the hour (blinds down, glowing, up on a dark or lit room); twelve ground-storey windows show and lead to other places (two-way). Opening a blind from inside a room is being taken (one-way, fixed per window), after which the window shows where and the room seals for three in-game days (saved).
- **The Windows (Entity 2):** about one room in sixteen, moving each in-game day, never into an occupied room: a figure beckons within 6 blocks; within 2 for a second the arm grabs, holds, hurts and drags; two blows or 3.5 blocks frees you; six seconds at the glass is death. A stability drain near one.
- **Peripherals:** a shape at the screen's edge, gone when you turn toward it; −0.02 stability each.
- **Air:** courtyard 8 °C, rooms 21 °C, stair 15 °C, roof 6 °C; stability 1.05 in rooms, 0.95 in corridors, 0.85 in the courtyard, 0.8 on the roof; danger 0.45 (Skin-Stealers, Smilers in the dark); safe in a guest room with its door shut.
- **The guest book:** open on the lobby's reception desk; right-click the desk to read it.
- **Items:** the housekeeping keycard (opens rooms that are only locked, never sealed ones), cereal, juice boxes; wardrobes, minibars, carts, linen shelves and beds salvage to cloth, clothes, Almond Water, juice and crackers. The building itself (walls, floors, windows, doors, stairs, the deck and the courtyard) never breaks (as the lore has it, forcing its doors has always failed).
